# Brand Identity
%% thumb: link-atlascloud-is-en.png

Dates: 26 Oct 2026 – 5 Feb 2027
Client: Atlas Cloud ehf.
Contact: Katla Ásgeirsdóttir

Brand identity for [Atlas](https://atlascloud.is/en), an Icelandic hosting, backup and disaster-recovery company, built as a [branded house](#media--lockup-system). M1 lays the foundation in naming, identity, voice and the website. M2 puts the system to use for UTmessan and hands every file over. M3, included in the fixed fee, sets Atlas up to make on-brand assets with Claude.

Alongside the website handoff, a free [SEO + GEO](#frontdoor) consultation covers search and AI visibility: metadata, structured data, crawler policy, domain and email records, and [analytics](https://metrics.kolkrabbi.io). Raised by Atlas in the first meeting, it comes with a [setup path](#frontdoor--implementation) the developer can follow step by step, independent of the build.

## M1 Foundation {#m1}

Dates: 26 Oct – 16 Dec 2026

[The foundation of the brand](https://merkelijkheid.nl/en/positioning/a-practical-view-on-brand-hierarchy-strategy-and-brand-architecture/). Naming, visual identity and verbal identity are developed together as one branded-house system, then applied to the website as the first real context. Everything is built into a [Figma library](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360041051154) and recorded in the initial brand guide, which carries the system forward into the fair materials of M2.

### 1.1 Kickoff {#kickoff}

Dates: 26 – 30 Oct

Acceptance: Both sides have confirmed the kickoff notes in writing.

Shared ground before any design work. The [brief](#brief) becomes a scope per milestone that both sides can hold to, with references collected, the market audited and a clear path for feedback and decisions agreed in writing.

#### [Visual references](#visuals)

Atlas's moodboard, plus brands and material they think are done well, from any industry. Their taste is on record before design starts, giving every feedback round a shared and concrete reference.

#### Competitor audit

How domestic and foreign hosting providers present themselves, so the identity can stand apart. It covers names, marks, colors and claims, and feeds the visual research in 1.3. The [brief](#brief)'s [avoid list](+avoid) applies.

[+avoid]: Avoid list
- Padlocks
- Shields
- Green binary code
- Hooded hackers
- Blue globes
- Vikings
- Flag colors

#### 📍 Sync: Kickoff session

Date: 26 Oct

The [brief](#brief) turned into a scope per milestone, with feedback rounds and the decision path agreed. The session also confirms who builds the website and when the booth goes to print.

#### Kickoff notes

Scope, dates, decision path, presentation date and the inputs needed from Atlas, sent the same day as the session. A written record both sides confirm before any design work begins.

### 1.2 Naming system {#name-review}

Dates: 26 Oct – 6 Nov

Acceptance: The naming routes are ready to present with the identity routes.

A naming logic for everything under one master brand, from products and solutions to resources and programs. The work is exploratory by design: it shows what is possible, not what is required, and is presented alongside the identity routes.

#### [Naming scope](#logo-system--live-lockup)

The master brand and the four levels beneath it, each needing a name or a naming rule:

- **Products:** readable backups, disaster recovery, hosting
- **Solutions:** data sovereignty, compliance, AI hosting
- **Resources:** incident reports, status, documentation
- **Programs:** partners, resellers, integrators

#### Naming sources

[Mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maat), the sciences, nature, the cosmos or plain descriptive names: any source giving every level a shared logic. [Atlas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_%28mythology%29) is already a titan in [Greek mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Themis), while [Norse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bifr%C3%B6st) sits close to the [brief](#brief)'s [avoid list](+avoid).[^fiction]

[^fiction]: Worth exploring in research: epic fiction gives a shared naming world without leaning on religion. Tolkien's [Númenor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%BAmenor) is his Atlantis, renamed Atalantë, the Downfallen, after it sank, and Frank Herbert's [Dune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dune_%28novel%29) novels build a whole universe of names: the planets Arrakis, Caladan, Giedi Prime, Kaitain and Ix, the machine world.

#### Naming language

Names that read the same in Icelandic and English need only one trademark and one set of files.[^tm] Atlas's view on language comes before any two-language versions of names are made.

[^tm]: Atlas is registered by Atlas Cloud ehf. in class 42, per the [brief](#brief).

### 1.3 Identity ✋ fifth element to confirm {#identity}

Dates: 9 – 27 Nov

Acceptance: Atlas signs off one route and the full system.

A branded-house identity: one visual system that works across every level of the organization. Built together with the naming in 1.2, it covers the [logo system](#media--brand-layout--logo-casedoc-01), color, typography and a [fifth element](#media--logo-construction--ref-structure-exmon-2) that carries the brand without the logo.

#### Visual research

Open visual research, with the hexagon and [basalt columns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columnar_jointing) as a starting point rather than a limit. Two or three routes are explored at concept stage, and one is refined.

#### Logo system

[Logomark](#media--logomarks--ref-logomarks-2) and wordmark, extended with [lockups](#media--lockups) and labels for every level of the brand hierarchy. The system makes clear at a glance where any product, solution, resource or program sits.

#### Color system

[Color ramps](#color--ramps) first, then a [palette](#media--color--ref-color-kolkrabbi-5) picked from them, then final swatches delivered as tokens. Key text and background pairs are contrast-checked for accessibility, and flag colors stay out, as the [brief](#brief) asks.

#### Type system

Typefaces chosen for character as much as function, tested in real layouts, in Icelandic and English, before the choice is made. They must work in the template tools Atlas uses.

A [type scale](#typography--ratio-generator) and [text styles](#typography--ratio-generator--ref-type-aftra) built on the chosen typefaces, from display headings to small labels. Both are delivered as tokens, so design files and code share the same values.

#### Icon system

A keyword [icon set](#media--icon-sets) drawn from the [brief](#brief) and refined alongside 1.4: cloud, data center, hosting, network, secure connection, locality. Icons are drawn or selected on the [logo geometry](#media--brand-layout--ref-logo-aftra-2), with these decisions settled first:

- **Style:** outline, solid or duotone
- **Color:** monochrome by default, color where it carries meaning
- **Detail:** size tiers from UI icon to spot illustration
- **Boundaries:** where an icon ends and a graphic or empty state begins

#### Fifth element

A device recognized without the logo; the [brief](#brief) leaves the other four to confirm. One direction pairs [Icelandic highlands](#media--visual-direction--ref-photo-highland-3) with [data-center hardware](#media--visual-direction--ref-photo-data-center-1): hosting as space, cold air and clean energy. Another is [product UI abstractions](#media--device-abstractions--device-comp-01).

#### Brand applications

Logo, type and color in layout for the first time: [business card](#media--stationery--ref-stationery-exmon), [letterhead](#media--stationery--ref-stationery-flik), [email signature](#media--stationery--ref-stationery-another), [roll-up](#media--mockups--ref-mockup-billboard) and [other mockups](#media--mockups--ref-mockup-package-crop). These prove the system works in use, while production files follow in M2.

#### 📍 Presentation: [Identity routes](#media--logomarks--ref-logomarks-3)

Date: 18 Nov

Naming routes and logo routes are presented together in one session, since each shapes the other. Atlas chooses a direction, which is then developed into the full system across every level.

### 1.4 Verbal identity {#message-critique}

Dates: 9 – 20 Nov

Acceptance: Atlas has agreed the notes and the website copy can be written from them.

How Atlas speaks, reviewed and sharpened from the material in the [brief](#brief) rather than rewritten. Key messages, voice, messaging style and values are tightened so words and visuals carry one identity across every channel and every audience.

#### [Key message](#brand-guide--key-messages)

A light review of the four key messages and their foreign-market version, sharpened where they read weakly. The results go straight into the mockups as real copy, not [placeholder text](https://vlad-saling.github.io/star-trek-ipsum/).

#### [Brand voice](#brand-guide--tone-of-voice)

How the brand sounds: calm and informative, friendly but not silly, with word choice and humor defined. Includes how to talk about cost and savings without implying cheap products or low quality.

#### [Copy style](#media--brand-layout--ref-moisall-15)

Headlines, captions and calls to action, and how a single message adapts across web, ads and decks. [Consistent patterns](#brand-guide--post-sizes) make every channel recognizably the same brand, whatever the format or length.

#### [Brand values](#brand-guide--about--ref-guide-flik-statement)

A review of the values and personality set out in the [brief](#brief), tightened and clarified where needed. The result guides every design and copy decision and forms the first chapter of the [brand guide](#brand-guide).

### 1.5 [Website design](#media--device-abstractions--devices-01) {#website-design}

Dates: 23 Nov – 11 Dec

Acceptance: Atlas signs off the pages and a developer can start from the Figma file.

The identity in its first real context. The site structure, pages and components are designed in Figma with responsive versions, and everything is organized into a working library that a developer can build from directly.

#### Site structure

What is online today and what the new site needs to do, summed up in one [sitemap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Site_map) that guides the design. Navigation follows the [brand hierarchy](#media--lockup-system): Products, Solutions, Resources and Programs.

#### Page design

Pages from the sitemap, designed in Figma with [responsive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Responsive_web_design) versions for the breakpoints agreed with Atlas. Buttons, forms, cards and status elements are collected as reusable components as the pages take shape.

#### Motion prototypes

Loaders, transitions and scroll behavior prototyped in code with [GSAP](https://gsap.com) or [Framer Motion](https://motion.dev): Figma can prototype motion, but not as it will run in development. The prototypes serve as developer reference, not production code.

#### [Figma library](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360041051154)

Tokens, color and type styles, icons, graphics and components, organized as one working library from the start. In M2, the same library is extended with templates and published to the team.

#### 📍 Demo: [Developer handoff](https://www.figma.com/dev-mode/)

Date: 11 Dec

The pages are walked through with Atlas and the developer in one session. The developer then takes over from the Figma file, with the library as the single source of truth.[^seo]

[^seo]: Offered alongside the handoff at no charge: a consultation on search and AI visibility, based on the [SEO + GEO](#frontdoor) checklist.

### 1.6 Brand guide ✋ deliverables, asset home and output format {#brand-guide}

Dates: 7 – 16 Dec

Acceptance: Delivered by mid-December and accepted; payment for M1 falls due.

[The system in one place](#media--brand-layout--ref-moisall-02): a living reference that records every rule, from brand values to the logo system and imagery. It is delivered with M1, linked to the Figma library, and grows through M2 as templates and applications are added.

#### Guide structure

Chapters are ordered by how the guide is used, not by the order the work was done:

1. Brand: values, personality, voice
2. [Branded house](#media--lockup-system): brand hierarchy, [logo system](#media--brand-layout--logo-casedoc-01), [lockups](#media--lockups)
3. Color: ramps, palette, tokens
4. Typography: typefaces, scale, text styles
5. Imagery: icons, fifth element, photography
6. Applications: website, templates, print

#### Usage rules

Where each part of the system applies and where it stops: [clear space](#media--clearspace), minimum sizes, color and type in use. Visual rules and correct and incorrect usage examples complete each chapter.

#### [Delivery formats](https://www.frontify.com/en/guide/why-you-need-digital-brand-guidelines)

What gets delivered, in which format, and where it lives, decided with Atlas:

- **Guide:** hosted brand site, Figma page or PDF
- **Logo exports:** screen and print, RGB and CMYK, each with a reason
- **Asset home:** where each lives and who maintains it

A hosted brand site is one proven option. Earlier clients have their identity summarized on its own subdomain, such as [brand.another-creation.xyz](https://brand.another-creation.xyz/styleguide) and [brand.kolkrabbi.io](https://brand.kolkrabbi.io), kept current alongside the Figma design files.

#### 📍 [Presentation](#brand-guide--slide-deck--ref-guide-another-slow): M1 delivery

Date: 16 Dec

The guide, website design and Figma library are walked through together in one session, closing M1. Feedback from this session shapes the templates and the brand guide update planned for M2.

## M2 Applications {#m2}

Dates: 4 Jan – 5 Feb 2027

The system in use. Everything Atlas needs for [UTmessan](https://utmessan.is) in February is produced from the M1 foundation: presentation and stationery templates, booth and print files, ads and social templates. A full handoff of the library, assets and files then lets the brand run without outside help, and M3 builds on it.

### 2.1 Templates ✋ template tool to confirm {#templates}

Dates: 4 – 15 Jan

Acceptance: Atlas can make a new deck from the template unaided.

Editable templates for what Atlas makes most often, built on the M1 system. Presentations and stationery move from mockup to production, in a format the Atlas team can edit day to day without a designer or specialist software.[^m3]

[^m3]: The templates are built agent-ready from the start, for M3. The same conversation settles how Atlas works with Claude: the app, [Claude Code](https://claude.com/product/claude-code) in a terminal, or [Claude Design](https://claude.com/product/design).

#### [Presentation template](#brand-guide--slide-deck)

Master slides with placeholder layouts for every common slide type: title, section, text, image, chart, comparison, quote, team and contact. Built in the tool Atlas chooses, so any presentation starts from the system.

#### Sales sheets

One-pager templates for sales material, from product sheets to case summaries, as the [brief](#brief) asks. Each sheet sets type, color and image areas, so the sales team fills in the content only.

#### Stationery files

[Business cards](#media--stationery--ref-stationery-exmon) and [letterhead](#media--stationery--ref-stationery-flik) from 1.3.7 turned into printer-ready files, with the right color profile, usually FOGRA39. The CMYK conversion of the palette is evaluated here, with spot colors where needed.

#### 📍 Demo: Template walkthrough

Date: 15 Jan

The templates walked through with the people at Atlas who will use them most. A new deck is built live from the template, which confirms the acceptance line for this phase.

### 2.2 Booth and print ✋ vendor deadline unknown {#booth-print}

Dates: 4 – 22 Jan

Acceptance: Files accepted and handed over in time for the vendor.

The identity at physical scale for UTmessan. The fair is a fixed date, so the vendor's print schedule sets every deadline in this phase, working back from the day the booth goes up in the hall.

#### [Roll-up banner](#media--mockups--ref-mockup-billboard)

The roll-up developed from the 1.3.7 mockup into a final design at full size. Legibility is checked at reading distance, since a banner works from several meters away, not up close.

#### Booth graphics

Backdrop and counter graphics designed for the booth size and layout Atlas confirms. The graphics work as one space with the roll-up, so the booth reads as a single brand from the aisle.

#### Print production

Printer-ready files for the roll-up and booth, with [bleed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleed_%28printing%29), color profile and the vendor's specifications. The CMYK checks from 2.1.3 carry over, and printing itself stays outside the agreed scope.

#### 📍 Sync: Print sign-off

Date: 22 Jan

A final check of every print file with Atlas before the files go to the vendor. Sign-off happens in time for the vendor's deadline, with a proof requested where the budget allows.

### 2.3 Ads and social {#ads-social}

Dates: 18 – 29 Jan

Acceptance: Atlas can produce a new ad from a template.

Templates, not campaigns. The structure and visual system are fixed in each template, while Atlas supplies the copy and images, so new ads and social posts stay on brand without needing design support every single time.

#### Ad templates

LinkedIn, Meta and Google display templates in the sizes and channels agreed at kickoff. Each template sets type, color and image areas, so only the message and the call to action change.

#### [Social templates](#brand-guide--post-sizes)

Templates for organic social posts on the channels agreed at kickoff, from announcements to incident updates. A small set of layouts covers most needs, keeping the feed consistent without repetition.

#### 📍 Demo: Template in use

Date: 29 Jan

A new ad is produced live from a template, with copy written by Atlas. The session shows where the template flexes and where the system holds, before the guide is updated.

### 2.4 Guide update {#guide-update}

Dates: 1 – 5 Feb

Acceptance: The guide matches the delivered files.

The guide extended with everything made in M2. Each new template and print file gets its rules and examples, so the guide matches the delivered files at handoff and stays the single reference long afterwards.

#### [Application rules](#brand-guide--stationery)

Rules and examples for presentations, stationery, print, booth, ads and social, added to the applications chapter. Atlas's feedback on the M2 templates shapes what each rule says and how strict it is.

### 2.5 Handoff {#handoff}

Dates: 1 – 5 Feb

Acceptance: Atlas downloads an asset and starts a new file from the library on their own.

Ownership moves to Atlas. The library, assets and files are organized and handed over so the brand runs without outside help, and the AI asset system in M3 then builds directly on this same foundation.

#### [Team library](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360025508373)

The Figma library from 1.5.4, extended with the M2 templates and published as a team library for Atlas. Needs a Figma plan that supports team libraries and shared component updates.

#### [Asset repository](#assets)

Logos, icons, photography and exports collected in one repository with a download table. Atlas decides who owns and maintains it after handoff, so the files stay current as the brand grows.

#### File delivery

How single files reach the people who need them, following the 1.6.3 decision: [brand site](https://brand.kolkrabbi.io), repository or Figma. Every format is checked against its primary use before the handoff session.

#### 📍 Teaching: Handoff session

Date: 5 Feb

An in-person session where the Atlas team downloads an asset and starts a new file from the library on their own. The session confirms the acceptance line and closes M2.

## M3 Automation {#m3}

Dates: 25 Jan – 5 Feb 2027
Price: Included

Brand rules become files Claude can read, the brand is set up as a design artifact with a few templates, and Claude is connected to the Figma library, so anyone at Atlas can type a short brief and get an on-brand draft without a designer. It builds on the M2 templates and brand guide, runs alongside the end of M2 and is included in the fixed fee.

### 3.1 Brand knowledge {#brand-knowledge}

Dates: 25 – 29 Jan

Acceptance: A fresh Claude session answers brand questions correctly from the files.

The brand guide translated into structured files Claude can read. Every tool and every session starts from the same rules, which makes this phase the shared foundation for everything else that then follows in M3.

#### Brand files

[Design tokens](https://www.designtokens.org/), logo rules and voice rules written once as structured files Claude can read. They mirror the brand guide, so a change in one is easy to carry into the other without duplication.

#### Agent setup

A brand [CLAUDE.md](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory) plus setup for the Claude surfaces Atlas uses: the app, Claude Code or [Claude Design](https://claude.com/product/design). Each surface loads the same brand files whenever a new session starts.

### 3.2 Design artifact {#design-artifact}

Dates: 25 Jan – 3 Feb

Acceptance: A text brief gives a first draft Atlas could publish after light edits.

The brand set up where Atlas works with Claude: one design artifact that holds the system, and a few templates built from it. Drafts start from the real brand, not from a description of it.

#### Design artifact

Atlas's design system as a [Claude Design](https://claude.com/product/design) artifact: tokens, type, logo and components from the M1 library. Every new design then starts on brand, on the Claude surface chosen in 2.1.

#### Brief templates

A few templates for what Atlas makes most often, such as an ad, a social post and a one-pager, built on the 2.1 and 2.3 templates. A short brief fills one in for review.

### 3.3 Figma connection {#figma-connection}

Dates: 1 – 5 Feb

Acceptance: A text brief produces an ad from library components in Figma.

Atlas's Claude connected to the [Figma team library](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360025508373) from 2.5. Drafts are built from real components and styles rather than imitations, so output matches the system exactly and stays in step as the library evolves.

#### Figma access

Claude connected to the Figma team library through the [Figma MCP](https://developers.figma.com/docs/figma-mcp-server/), tested early on Atlas's plan. Access is scoped to the library, so generation reads components without ever changing them.

#### 📍 Teaching: Walkthrough session

Date: 5 Feb

Part of the M2 handoff session: the team produces an asset from a text brief, from first prompt to final export. The session closes M3 and confirms the acceptance line for this milestone.
