# Five brands compared
%% thumb: rijksmuseum-2012-unveiling.jpg

Covers: Cloudflare, Rijksmuseum, Sanity, Vercel, Wieden+Kennedy

**What do five very different brands have in common?** Not their colours or their typefaces, but how they hold them. Each identity changed when the business changed, not when taste did. Each look rests on three or four decisions kept for years, usually one colour or none, type that carries the hierarchy, and one device built from visible construction. Each also leaves the same things unwritten: the idea, the logo rules, the voice.

The five answered the same questions: the idea behind the brand, who made the identity and why, how logo, colour, type and voice work, where the marketing happens, and how it was received. [Cloudflare](https://www.cloudflare.com/) and [Vercel](https://vercel.com/) are developer platforms. [Sanity](https://www.sanity.io/) is a content platform with a new identity from 2025. The [Rijksmuseum](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en) is a national museum and [Wieden+Kennedy](https://www.wk.com/) an ad agency. The spread is the point: where all five agree, the pattern holds beyond them.

## The idea behind the brand

Few brands write their idea down in one sentence. Two did, and in both cases the designer wrote it.

> Where there is chaos, there must be structure. — [Bleed](https://bleed.com/work/sanity), on Sanity

> A national museum with international appeal. — [Irma Boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irma_Boom), on the Rijksmuseum

Vercel has no sentence. Its idea is carried by what it leaves out: a monochrome palette, one triangle, no mascot. Its design studio [Basement](https://basement.studio/post/scaling-vercel-years-of-building-refining-and-elevating) put it as "Vercel is all about performance. Their brand had to match." Cloudflare has a mission, "help build a better Internet", and its agency's concept, ["boldly go"](https://allati.co/project/cloudflare-brand-refresh-2020), and the two do not say the same thing. Wieden+Kennedy's idea is its founder's maxims, typed on every page of its site.

> The work comes first. Walk in stupid every morning. — [Dan Wieden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Wieden)

## What set the change off

Every identity change in the set followed a business event. Vercel [renamed from ZEIT](https://vercel.com/blog/zeit-is-now-vercel) when it raised its first big round in 2020. Sanity launched its identity on the day it launched a new product and announced funding, in May 2025, because the old name for what it sold no longer fit.

> I think we're not a headless CMS. — [Magnus Hillestad](https://cmscritic.com/sanity-says-cmses-are-broken-is-its-content-operating-system-the-next-evolutionary-step), Sanity

The Rijksmuseum needed an identity for its [2013 reopening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijksmuseum) after ten years closed. Cloudflare [flattened its logo in 2016](https://blog.cloudflare.com/time-for-an-update/) for a practical reason: the gradient was "really hard to reproduce on t-shirts, signs, stickers". Wieden+Kennedy's wordmark has not changed since at least 2011, and no one has ever written a brief for it.

## Who made it, and how long it lasts

The Rijksmuseum and Sanity are agency work: [Irma Boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irma_Boom) with the type foundry [Bold Monday](https://www.boldmonday.com/en/rijksmuseum/), and [Bleed](https://bleed.com/) in Oslo. Vercel designs in-house with a long-running studio partner, [Basement](https://basement.studio/post/scaling-vercel-years-of-building-refining-and-elevating). Cloudflare's logo has passed through [three sets of hands](https://blog.cloudflare.com/rejected-cloudflare-logos) in fifteen years: a logo designer in 2010, the in-house team in 2016, a creative director in 2020. Wieden+Kennedy credits no one.

Whoever made them, the marks last. Each logo in the set has held its structure for fourteen to sixteen years, while the websites, palettes and typefaces under them changed several times. Keep words that go stale out of the mark.

## More than one audience

Every brand speaks to more than one audience. The useful ones say which comes first.

> Cloudflare is for everyone. — The opening line of its [content style guide](https://cf-assets.www.cloudflare.com/dzlvafdwdttg/27kv7mXCSjQj5HdOXvgkhK/0962567dbf49ec41f6d2f48cea2b3653/BDES-8129_CF_Content_Style_Guide_2025_RD3.pdf), before it names seven groups

Sanity's brief named two, the marketing executive and the developer. Most of the Rijksmuseum's visitors [come from abroad](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/press/press-releases/the-rijksmuseum-in-2024), while its stated priority is young adults and families at home. Vercel's audience has widened in three steps, from JavaScript developers to front-end teams to, now, ["people and agents"](https://vercel.com/about). Wieden+Kennedy names clients and talent and ranks neither.

![[rijksmuseum-family-month.jpg|[Rijksmuseum](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en): Family Month, for the audience at home]]
![[sanity-event-nyc.jpg|[Sanity](https://www.sanity.io/): a conference for the developer audience]]

## What the designers say they did

When a reason is published, it is about the type or the symbol, never the colour. Vercel explains [Geist](https://vercel.com/font), its typeface, through Swiss modernism and speed, and says nothing about why black and white.

> The target is Vercel judgment, not Vercel decoration. — Vercel's [design guidelines](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines), 2026

The Rijksmuseum's reasons are typographic, down to reviving the [Dutch IJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IJ_%28digraph%29) as one letter. Cloudflare's first designer [changed the E](https://blog.cloudflare.com/rejected-cloudflare-logos) in the wordmark so it would not look like Oracle's. Sanity's reasons are its agency's case study. Wieden+Kennedy has none.

![[rijksmuseum-signage.jpg|[Rijksmuseum](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en): lettering in the house typeface]]

## How they were received

Only the Rijksmuseum got real design criticism, and the space in RIJKS MUSEUM drew hundreds of complaints to a Dutch spelling watchdog.

> A vast typographic improvement. A fine design. But nothing more. — [Brand New](https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/rijksmuseum.php), 2012

Sanity has [award juries](https://www.grafill.no/visuelt/vinnere/2026/grafisk-design/identitetsdesign-stor/identitet-for-innholdsplattform-sanity), Vercel has forum reaction to the new name, Cloudflare has press about its [lava lamps](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/ssl/lava-lamp-encryption/), and Wieden+Kennedy is [judged as an agency](https://www.adweek.com/agencies/wieden-kennedy-adweek-2025-global-agency-of-the-year/), not as an identity.

## Where the marketing happens

The developer brands market through their own events. Vercel runs [Ship](https://vercel.com/ship) and [Next.js Conf](https://nextjs.org/conf) and has never run a classic ad campaign. Sanity launched its identity with screens on Times Square and then held its own conference. Cloudflare alone has run named ad campaigns, [in 2018](https://blog.cloudflare.com/betterinternet-join-the-movement) and 2020. The Rijksmuseum's biggest moments were [sponsor stunts](https://www.banken.nl/nieuws/562/ing-video-rijksmuseum-trekt-internationaal-de-aandacht), including a supermarket's milk cartons for the reopening. Wieden+Kennedy's marketing is its clients' ads.

## Off screen

The memorable physical things are functional or architectural, not decorative. Cloudflare's [wall of lava lamps](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/ssl/lava-lamp-encryption/) feeds its encryption. The Rijksmuseum identity sits on the building, and four of its nine custom fonts were drawn for signage. Wieden+Kennedy's brand lives in its [Portland warehouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wieden%2BKennedy). Vercel and Sanity have event sets and merchandise.

## One master brand

All five are [master brands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand_architecture). Products get a name, and only sometimes a logo. Sanity's products have no logos at all. The Rijksmuseum adds a noun to its name. Cloudflare puts its name in front of most products and lets a few coined names stand alone. Wieden+Kennedy tags its offices with a plus and a city code, and London spun off a design studio called NOT Wieden+Kennedy. Vercel is the exception, with [six marked brands](https://vercel.com/geist/brands) and a logo pack for each.

## Guides and design systems

The marketing brand guide is rare and the UI design system is common, the opposite of what a branding project might expect. Four of the five publish a [design system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_system) for their product interfaces. Only two publish a brand guide.

| Brand | Brand guide | Design system |
|---|---|---|
| Cloudflare | [Full guide](https://www.cloudflare.com/brand/): logo, colour, type, design elements, illustration, photography, plus a [content style guide](https://cf-assets.www.cloudflare.com/dzlvafdwdttg/27kv7mXCSjQj5HdOXvgkhK/0962567dbf49ec41f6d2f48cea2b3653/BDES-8129_CF_Content_Style_Guide_2025_RD3.pdf) | [Kumo](https://kumo-ui.com/), open source |
| Rijksmuseum | None | [Styleguide](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/styleguide), UI only |
| Sanity | None, only a [logo package](https://github.com/sanity-io/logos) with no rules | [Sanity UI](https://www.sanity.io/ui) |
| Vercel | [One page](https://vercel.com/geist/brands): symbol rules, spacing, a misuse list, download packs | [Geist](https://vercel.com/geist/introduction), 72 components |
| Wieden+Kennedy | None | None |

The brand guides and the live sites drift apart. Cloudflare's guide names one typeface and one orange while the site runs another typeface and a brighter orange. The Rijksmuseum styleguide's colour names appear nowhere in its code. Vercel keeps its guide beside its design system, and drifts least.

## Voice

Voice is the least documented layer. Two of the five write it down, and both sites break their own rules.

| Brand | Written down? | How it sounds | Words it repeats |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cloudflare | Yes: for everyone, bold, helpful, with a long [style guide](https://cf-assets.www.cloudflare.com/dzlvafdwdttg/27kv7mXCSjQj5HdOXvgkhK/0962567dbf49ec41f6d2f48cea2b3653/BDES-8129_CF_Content_Style_Guide_2025_RD3.pdf) | Jokes on developer pages, jargon for enterprise | build, by default, Internet with a capital I |
| Rijksmuseum | No | A warm host, questions and exclamation marks | masterpieces; Rembrandt, Vermeer and Van Gogh |
| Sanity | No | Fragments in threes, "this, not that" | content operations |
| Vercel | Yes: copy rules in its [interface guidelines](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines) | Headlines under eight words, proof as numbers | ship, deploy |
| Wieden+Kennedy | No, but [ten maxims](https://www.wk.com/) typed on every page | First person plural, self-deprecating | the work, independent |

> We were founded as an ad agency that didn't like advertising. — Wieden+Kennedy

The platform voices are terse and claim-led. The museum writes as a host. The agency understates and keeps its humour for the edges, the 404 page and the legal page. One word turned up at Cloudflare, Vercel and Sanity at once in 2026: agent. That is where all three are moving their positioning.

## Colour

Four of the five palettes fit in a sentence. Cloudflare's does not, and it is also the only brand that [documents its colours](https://www.cloudflare.com/brand/color/) fully, with print values.

| Brand | Core colours | How much of the page | Dark mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cloudflare | Tangerine, Ruby, Mango and white, plus six secondaries | Orange as whole surfaces | Follows the system |
| Rijksmuseum | Slate, black, white, and a red used for tickets only | A little red, the rest neutral | Dark only |
| Sanity | Near-black and one orange | The orange as an accent | Follows the system |
| Vercel | Black, white and [ten greys](https://vercel.com/geist/colors) | None | A switch |
| Wieden+Kennedy | Black and white | None | Light only |

The brands differ most on how much area the brand colour gets. Vercel's whole identity is the absence of colour. Sanity shows its orange in the monogram and on hover. Cloudflare commits a full orange card to the top of its homepage. The Rijksmuseum has a second palette nobody sees: six colours drawn from each of eighteen paintings, used on wallpaper and merchandise but gone from the site.

Few of the five state an accessibility target. The most honest statement in the set is a failing one: the [Rijksmuseum styleguide](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/styleguide) lists which of its colour pairs pass and which do not.

## Logo

Four of the five logos are a wordmark with at most one extra element.

| Brand | The mark | Colour | Rules published |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cloudflare | A two-part cloud with a flare streak, heavy rounded capitals | Two oranges | [In full](https://www.cloudflare.com/brand/): clear space, minimum sizes, what to avoid |
| Rijksmuseum | A drawn wordmark in bold capitals, with a deliberate space and the Dutch IJ | Black or white, with an R mark for apps | None |
| Sanity | A drawn serif wordmark and a jagged symbol, the squiggle | The symbol on orange | None |
| Vercel | A solid triangle and a logotype | Black or white only, no colour version | [In part](https://vercel.com/geist/brands): clear space, an eight-item misuse list |
| Wieden+Kennedy | Wieden over Kennedy in light [Univers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Univers), with a plus hung off the right | Black or white | None |

Every wordmark is either drawn for the brand or set in a [neo-grotesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sans-serif#Neo-grotesque). Only Cloudflare's mark is multicoloured. The two brands with the best published rules are the two with full brand guides. Variants run from one lockup to a dozen: Cloudflare publishes stacked, horizontal, partner, programme and team lockups; Vercel publishes black and white only; Wieden+Kennedy's variants are scattered across its offices.

## Type

Two of the five own their typeface. Vercel commissioned [Geist](https://vercel.com/font) from [Basement](https://basement.studio/post/the-birth-of-geist-a-typeface-crafted-for-the-web) in three cuts, Sans, Mono and Pixel, and released it as [open source](https://github.com/vercel/geist-font), so it has spread well beyond the brand. The Rijksmuseum had [Bold Monday](https://www.boldmonday.com/en/rijksmuseum/) draw a family of nine fonts, four of them for signage, with the Dutch IJ revived as one letter. The other three license.

| Brand | Typeface | Own or licensed | How it is used |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cloudflare | [Inter](https://rsms.me/inter/) in the [guide](https://www.cloudflare.com/brand/typography/), STK Bureau Sans on the site | Licensed | Medium headings, all-caps eyebrows |
| Rijksmuseum | Rijksmuseum, nine fonts by Bold Monday | Own | Bold uppercase headings, two weights only |
| Sanity | Waldenburg, with [IBM Plex Mono](https://www.ibm.com/plex/) for labels | Licensed | Light headings, mono uppercase labels and buttons |
| Vercel | Geist Sans, Mono and Pixel | Own, open source | Light headings, tightened at display sizes |
| Wieden+Kennedy | [Univers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Univers) | Licensed | Light weight everywhere, hierarchy by size alone |

All five sit in the [Swiss grotesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sans-serif#Grotesque) line. Sanity and Vercel pair a sans with a mono; the other three run one family.

Three of the five get hierarchy from size and spacing, not weight. Vercel, Sanity and Wieden+Kennedy keep every heading light and tighten the spacing as the type gets bigger. Cloudflare sits in between, with medium headings. The Rijksmuseum is the outlier: two weights, every heading bold and uppercase, set tight, and it can do that because the typeface is its own. Colour almost never touches type anywhere in the set; emphasis comes from scale, grey or inversion. Cloudflare's cream headline on an orange field is the one exception.

Only Vercel publishes its [type scale](https://vercel.com/geist/typography). For the other four, the scale comes from reading the site. The quiet difference is body size: Wieden+Kennedy sets its body at 20 px in the light weight, Cloudflare at 16.

## The signature device

Beyond logo, colour and type, each brand has one device, and three of the five are built from construction.

| Brand | The device |
|---|---|
| Cloudflare | [The flare](https://www.cloudflare.com/brand/design-elements/): arcs taken from the logomark, plus dashed hairlines and a dot grid |
| Rijksmuseum | The wordmark at full width across a masterpiece, and the Dutch IJ |
| Sanity | A library of dot and line textures, 36 of them, built into the code |
| Vercel | [Hairline grid guides](https://vercel.com/geist/grid) with plus marks at the intersections, and the triangle as an object |
| Wieden+Kennedy | The plus as a word: in the logo, the monogram, the office tags |

> Never a complete flare. — Cloudflare's rule for its arcs, with at most two arcs and a crop of a fifth or more

Devices with written rules survive in other people's hands: write the rules down. Cloudflare and Sanity arrived at near-identical dot patterns independently.

## Imagery and icons

Imagery follows what the brand has: the collection for the museum, the clients' work for the agency, abstract renders for Vercel and Sanity, product screens and illustration for Cloudflare. Only Cloudflare [writes its imagery rules down](https://www.cloudflare.com/brand/). Vercel's core pages show no photography of people at all. Icons are the one place most brands borrow: three [draw their own set](https://vercel.com/geist/icons), one uses a library, the agency draws one-offs.
