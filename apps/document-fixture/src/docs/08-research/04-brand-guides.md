# Brand guides: what they contain, how they are ordered and how they state rules
%% thumb: bg-slack-p22-logo-misuse.png

Covers: Dropbox, GitHub, Mailchimp, Mozilla, NASA 1975, Slack, Uber, Wise

**What does a useful brand guide contain?** A logo chapter with exact rules, and a short strategy turned into instructions. The guides people actually use measure clear space in a part of the mark, put a wrong example beside every prohibition, give a reason beside every rule, and ship the files with the rules. Most of the rest is optional, and most guides leave something essential out.

This page reads eight published guides as documents: which chapters they carry, in what order, how deep each goes, how the rules are worded, and what is missing. They span fifty years, from [NASA's 1975 manual](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/nasa_graphics_manual_nhb_1430-2_jan_1976.pdf), a ring binder, to [Wise's](https://wise.design) website, which holds brand and product in one place. Between them sit [Slack's](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0565/3423/7349/files/slack-2020.pdf) 50-page PDF, the [Dropbox](https://brand.dropbox.com) and [GitHub](https://brand.github.com) portals, [Uber's](https://web.archive.org/web/20190421000350/https://brand.uber.com/guide) system site, [Mozilla's](https://web.archive.org/web/20190721162600/https://mozilla.design/mozilla/) short website and [Mailchimp's](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/) writing guide.

## What chapters guides contain

The logo is the only chapter all eight have. Strategy, colour and type follow in seven. Voice, illustration and motion appear in six, photography, icons and applications in five. Accessibility and co-branding appear in four, templates and digital rules in three. Sound appears in none.

NASA is the odd one out: no strategy chapter, and its colours and typefaces sit inside the logotype and publications sections rather than in chapters of their own.

### Having a chapter says little

The same chapter can be one sentence or a measured specification. Mailchimp's logo page is two sentences and three images; Slack's logo content runs nine pages. Colour is the same story. Wise, Uber, Mozilla and GitHub give every colour in screen and print values. Dropbox gives full values for three core colours and shows the rest only on hover. Mailchimp gives its two colours inside an image. NASA gives two physical swatches and no numbers at all.

| Guide | Colour values | Logo clear space | Minimum logo size |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Wise](https://web.archive.org/web/20250603145203/https://wise.design/foundations/logo) | Screen and print, with contrast scores | One flag | For screen and print |
| [Uber](https://web.archive.org/web/20210417045006/https://brand.uber.com/us/en/logo/) | Screen and print | The cap height of the U | For screen |
| [Mozilla](https://web.archive.org/web/20190721162402/https://mozilla.design/mozilla/logo-usage/) | Screen and print | Half the mark, in a figure | For screen and print |
| [Slack](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0565/3423/7349/files/slack-2020.pdf#page=18) | Screen and print | Worded three different ways | For screen |
| [GitHub](https://brand.github.com/foundations/logo) | Screen and print | A share of the mark | None |
| [Dropbox](https://brand.dropbox.com/logo) | Full for three colours, the rest on hover | None stated | None |
| [Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/about/brand-assets/) | Screen only, inside an image | "Plenty of space" | None |

### Voice is where guides differ most

Either voice is the whole guide, or a few pages, or a link to somewhere else. [Mailchimp's content style guide](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/) is the extreme: seventeen sections, almost all of it about writing. Wise's voice pages are the deepest part of its site. Uber has a full chapter with editing tips, Slack three pages, Dropbox one page with four pillars. GitHub's voice page links out to a private document that does not open. Mozilla and NASA have no voice chapter.

> We break a number of grammar rules for clarity, practicality, or preference. — [Mailchimp content style guide](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/)

![[bg-site-mailchimp-styleguide.png|[Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/about/brand-assets/): the whole guide is about writing]]
![[bg-uber-toneOfVoice-img-01.png|[Uber](https://brand.uber.com/): tone of voice, shown as a sample email]]
### Strategy matters only when it becomes a rule

Strategy chapters are short everywhere but Slack, which spends eleven pages on mission, problem, six values and a persona, and never ties a value to a colour, shape or typeface. Dropbox's strategy is one screen: a problem and four pillars. The guides that make strategy useful turn each trait into an instruction.

| Guide | How a trait becomes a rule |
|---|---|
| [GitHub](https://brand.github.com/brand-identity/attributes) | Five attributes, each with copy, design and event rules |
| [Mozilla](https://web.archive.org/web/20190721162528/https://mozilla.design/mozilla/personality/) | Four personality words, each with a design line |
| [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/nasa_graphics_manual_nhb_1430-2_jan_1976.pdf#page=5) | One paragraph ties the letterforms to what they mean |
| [Uber](https://web.archive.org/web/20190722141102/https://brand.uber.com/brand-story) | Each system section opens by tying back to the brand story |
| [Wise](https://web.archive.org/web/20250422202257/https://wise.design/foundations/mission) | Mission copy to reuse word for word; the strategy itself is behind a sign-in |

> Embrace monospace fonts and code-inspired elements. — [GitHub](https://brand.github.com/brand-identity/attributes), on the attribute "Nerdy"

> Be playful, be weird, appeal to the imagination. — [Mozilla](https://web.archive.org/web/20190721162528/https://mozilla.design/mozilla/personality/), on "Optimistic"

![[bg-site-github-attributes.png|[GitHub](https://brand.github.com/): each attribute as copy, design and event rules]]

## Order

Most guides open with the story or an introduction and close with legal, downloads and contacts. The logo comes first only in the older, mark-centred guides.

| Guide | Opens with | The logo comes | Ends with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dropbox | Its strategy | Third of eight | Partner toolkit and legal |
| GitHub | Getting started, then attributes | Eighth of twenty-five | Motion |
| Mailchimp | Writing goals and principles | On one screen | Further reading, then a summary |
| Mozilla | About, then personality | Third of six | Typography |
| NASA | A three-page introduction | Section one | Miscellaneous and supplements |
| Slack | Who we are | Page seventeen | Governance, then "Questions?" |
| Uber | The brand story, then the logo | First system section | Downloads and help |
| Wise | Design at Wise | Inside an A–Z list | Downloads, partnerships, swag |

![[bg-nasa-logotype-crop.png|[NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/brand-guidelines/): section one is the logotype]]
![[bg-site-dropbox-framework.png|[Dropbox](https://brand.dropbox.com/): it opens with the problem, not the logo]]
### Slack's three parts

Slack's structure is the clearest model: define the brand, then the design elements, then governance. Each section opens with one sentence of purpose, and full-page illustrations with no text act as breaks. Governance is written as numbered legal clauses and doubles as the public trademark policy. Dropbox splits the same three parts across media: story on the site, specifications in the partner PDF, legal on a separate page.

![[bg-slack-p03-contents.png|[Slack](https://slack.com/media-kit): define, design, govern, in fifty pages]]
![[bg-slack-p44-trademarks.png|[Slack](https://slack.com/media-kit): governance, written as legal clauses]]
### No fixed order for logo, colour, type

Mozilla, Slack and Uber run logo, colour, type in that order. Dropbox runs logo, typography, icons, colour. GitHub sorts its sections alphabetically, so accessibility comes before colour and "Overview" lands last in Motion. Uber and Wise are mostly alphabetical too, which puts the logo and the colours alongside padding and radius. Mailchimp is the counter-example: it numbers every file to fix the order.

![[bg-site-github-toolkit.png|[GitHub](https://brand.github.com/): Foundations in alphabetical order]]
## Depth

Space goes where the owner most needs control: to the mark and its reproduction in the mark-centred guides, to words in the writing-centred ones, to layout at Uber.

| Guide | Where the space goes |
|---|---|
| NASA | Publications, reproduction art and the logotype; the logotype and its artwork take a third of the manual |
| Slack | The logo, then governance; colour gets two pages |
| Dropbox | The logo takes a third of the partner PDF |
| Mozilla | Logo and usage is longer than every other chapter together; visuals get one sentence |
| Uber | Composition, the layout section, is the longest |
| Mailchimp | Grammar, writing for translation, copyright |
| Wise | Grammar and style, colour, vocabulary, motion |
| GitHub | Motion, mascots, events and the logo; a quarter of the PDF is sub-brands |

![[bg-dropbox-p05-logo-tab.png|[Dropbox](https://brand.dropbox.com/): the Tab, glyph and plane]]
![[bg-dropbox-p09-tab-scales.png|[Dropbox](https://brand.dropbox.com/): the Tab scales with its content]]
![[bg-uber-composition-img-30.png|[Uber](https://brand.uber.com/): composition, its longest section]]
### Applications take over only in the oldest guide

More than half of NASA's manual is applications: publications, stationery, forms, signage, vehicles, decals, each specified to a fraction of an inch. The newer guides show applications as examples (GitHub's event booths, Dropbox's partner lockup) or as writing rules (Mailchimp's newsletters). Uber closes each section with a list of applications and keeps its templates behind a login.

![[bg-nasa-p45-signage.png|[NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/brand-guidelines/): signage, every sign specified]]
![[bg-github-pdf-p84-experiential-booths.png|[GitHub](https://brand.github.com/): event booths, as six principles]]
### Thin pages tell you something too

GitHub's presentations page is a staff-only link and its web page says "Samples coming soon." Wise shows an intro line and "Sign in" for its strategy, illustration and downloads. Mailchimp's current brand assets page has no strategy at all. What a guide leaves thin tells you what its owner keeps private.

## How rules are stated

The mark gets absolute rules with a wrong example beside each. Imagery and motion get principles.

> The logotype should never be altered or distorted in any way. It must not be re-drawn. — [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/nasa_graphics_manual_nhb_1430-2_jan_1976.pdf#page=10)

> Always use the logo files provided. Do not re-create. — [Slack](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0565/3423/7349/files/slack-2020.pdf#page=22)

| Guide | How the mark is ruled | Where it loosens |
|---|---|---|
| NASA | Nine numbered plates of wrong uses | It offers its signs as models to follow |
| Mozilla | "Do not remove the highlight from the letters, ever." Six struck-through logos | Imagery is used only with permission |
| Slack | Ten captioned don'ts on one page, repeated as legal clauses | Voice: "some of the rules are a little firm" |
| Uber | "Text should never be colored." Every section ends in a list of don'ts | Photography came as a shoot brief |
| GitHub | Six logo don'ts as image pairs | Illustration: the story matters more than consistency |
| Dropbox | A text-only list of prohibitions on the legal page | Recommendations and principles |
| Wise | A short misuse list, nearly every rule with a do and don't image | Motion: "These aren't rules, but tips" |
| Mailchimp | Short imperatives with yes and no pairs | Visuals: "just give them the chance to breathe" |

![[bg-nasa-incorrect-uses-crop.png|[NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/brand-guidelines/): nine incorrect uses]]
![[bg-slack-p22-logo-misuse.png|[Slack](https://slack.com/media-kit): ten don'ts on one page]]
![[bg-wise-logo-misuse-lockup.png|[Wise](https://wise.design/): don't change the lockups]]
### Measured in the mark itself

The best-specified guides state clear space in a part of the mark, so a designer needs no ruler. Uber's clear space is the cap height of the U. Wise's is one flag. Slack draws its logo on a grid and states clear space in parts of the mark. NASA builds its logotype so that three units equal one stroke, and measures clearance in strokes. Slack is also the weak case: its own wording for clear space changes across its editions.

> One flag's worth of clear space in all directions. — [Wise](https://web.archive.org/web/20250603145203/https://wise.design/foundations/logo)

![[bg-uber-logo-img-03.png|[Uber](https://brand.uber.com/): the cap height of the U]]

![[bg-nasa-p11-grid-drawing.png|[NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/brand-guidelines/): three units to a stroke]]
![[bg-mozilla-03-logo-clearspace-minsize-scaling.png|[Mozilla](https://brand.mozilla.com/): half the mark, and a minimum size]]
### Exact numbers, in any tone of voice

The tone of a guide does not decide its precision. Wise and Mailchimp write in a chatty voice, Uber and NASA in a flat one, and the numbers are exact in both. Wise follows a joke with millimetres and pixels. Uber states its limits as counts: no more than two motion states, no more than three colours in a composition. GitHub is the odd one: some of its numbers exist only inside images, not in the text.

> Money doesn't need borders. Logos do. — [Wise](https://web.archive.org/web/20250603145203/https://wise.design/foundations/logo)

### A reason with each rule

Give a reason with each rule: the guides that do are the ones designers call usable. NASA explains its decisions in plain language, down to why one typeface needs compensating. Uber files its reasons as don'ts. Mailchimp explains its rules and follows them. Slack is the opposite pattern: full specifications, few explanations.

> Restricting use to only black or white affords us the highest contrast ratio. — [Uber](https://web.archive.org/web/20210417060429/https://brand.uber.com/us/en/color/)

![[bg-nasa-helvetica-crop.png|[NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/brand-guidelines/): the typeface, with its reasons]]
![[bg-uber-color-img-33.png|[Uber](https://brand.uber.com/): black or white, for the highest contrast]]
## Format and scale

The eight come in three forms: a PDF or binder, a website, or a site paired with a PDF. The pairing separates the story from the specification.

| Guide | Form | Written for |
|---|---|---|
| NASA 1975 | A loose-leaf binder, now a scanned PDF | NASA designers, as official policy |
| Slack 2020 | A 50-page PDF | Customers, developers, partners, press |
| Dropbox 2025 | A web portal and a partner PDF | Anyone building in the Dropbox ecosystem |
| GitHub 2026 | A web portal, an 89-page PDF and a Figma file | Staff and outside collaborators |
| Uber 2018 | A website and a ten-slide partner PDF | Staff, agencies, partners |
| Mozilla 2019 | A website only | Anyone using the brand |
| Wise 2023 | One website for brand and product | Designers, and partners under resources |
| Mailchimp | Three web documents | Content teams |

Dropbox keeps the story and short chapters on its site and the exact values in its PDF. Uber does the same: the website for the people building the system, the PDF for partners and sponsors.

> Uber has always relied on PDFs … they were cumbersome to revise. — [Red Dot](https://www.red-dot.org/), on the 2015 Uber guide

### Most guides do not survive

Five of the eight no longer exist in the edition this research read.

| Guide | What happened |
|---|---|
| Slack | The web brand center went under construction and now returns nothing |
| Mozilla | The site redirects to a new portal with the 2024 identity |
| Uber | The site redirects to a portal behind a login |
| Wise | The site was rebuilt, and its foundations pages now render empty |
| NASA | Rescinded in 1992; the [Brand Center](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/brand-guidelines/) now allows the logotype only beside the insignia |
| Dropbox, GitHub | Live when read |
| Mailchimp | The content guide is live; the 2018 microsite redirects |

![[bg-site-slack-media-kit.png|[Slack](https://slack.com/media-kit) today: a media kit in place of the guide]]
![[bg-site-mozilla-today.png|[Mozilla](https://brand.mozilla.com/) today: a portal for the 2024 identity]]
### Product rules are rare

Only three guides have a digital chapter: GitHub, Mailchimp (as copy rules) and Wise. Wise puts brand and product on the same pages, "from button to billboard", so a designer gets the logo and the spacing rule from one source. GitHub hands product UI to its separate design system, [Primer](https://primer.style/). Slack, Dropbox, Mozilla and NASA state no interface rules.

![[bg-wise-type-wise-sans-intro.png|[Wise](https://wise.design/): the brand typeface in the product]]
![[bg-wise-type-wise-sans-success.png|[Wise](https://wise.design/): a success screen]]
### Gating moves the valuable assets out

Uber's files need an Uber login. GitHub's slide template is for staff only. Wise gates its strategy, illustration, patterns and downloads. Slack's downloads were behind a login. Dropbox ships one public zip with logos and the PDF, but no fonts. The public guide becomes a rulebook without its tools.

![[bg-site-wise-2025.png|[Wise](https://wise.design/), 2025: illustration and downloads behind a lock]]
![[bg-site-uber-today.png|[Uber](https://brand.uber.com/) today: the guide behind a sign-in]]
## What is commonly missing

| Gap | Where it shows |
|---|---|
| Sound | None of the eight; Uber and Wise say so |
| Minimum logo size | Missing in Dropbox, GitHub, Mailchimp; NASA fixes sizes per application |
| A named contrast standard | Slack says "pass the contrast standards" without naming one; Uber, Mozilla and Dropbox say nothing |
| Motion timings | GitHub and Wise give none; Uber gives durations but no easing |
| Where to get the fonts | Dropbox, Wise, Slack |
| A type scale and fallback fonts | Dropbox, Mailchimp, Uber, Wise |
| A changelog | None of the eight; only Uber dates its sections and NASA lists the pages in effect |
| Grid and layout rules | Dropbox, Mailchimp, Mozilla, Slack |

Accessibility is the clearest half-done case. Four guides have a chapter, and only Wise publishes contrast scores for its colours.

![[bg-slack-p26-accessible-colour.png|[Slack](https://slack.com/media-kit): accessible pairs, no standard named]]
![[bg-github-pdf-p54-text-contrast.png|[GitHub](https://brand.github.com/): text colour checked for contrast]]
### Contradictions

Six of the eight contradict themselves, and every time it is the same rule living in two places.

| Guide | Contradiction |
|---|---|
| Uber | A colour value that does not match its own other value, and values that changed between editions |
| GitHub | Different grey scales on the site and in the PDF; one page forbids resizing text, another calls the sizes a starting point |
| Slack | Clear space worded three ways; the 2020 PDF still says "Copyright 2019" |
| Mailchimp | One page capitalises every word on buttons, another uses sentence case |
| Dropbox | The site and the PDF give the name "Version A" to two different logos |
| Mozilla | A print value that does not describe its screen colour |

NASA and Wise have none on record. GitHub also breaks its own advice: it tells readers to tag PDFs for screen readers, and its own PDF is untagged.
