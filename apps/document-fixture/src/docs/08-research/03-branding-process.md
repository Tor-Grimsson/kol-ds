# How branding projects work
%% thumb: bp-airbnb-belo-sketch.png

Covers: Airbnb, Burger King, Liquid Death, Mailchimp, Mastercard, Mother, Radiohead, The Guardian, Whitney

**How does a branding project actually run?** It starts with a change in the business, not a matter of taste. The research looks for one sentence the work can hang on, the identity keeps what the brand already owns, and the rules decide what stays fixed and what is free. The launch draws a loud reaction, and the business results are claimed long before anyone can measure them.

Nine projects show it, from a payments network to a band, read across ten steps: the brief, research, strategy, the message and the pitch, voice, visual identity, mood, rollout, the guide, and the reception. Most of what is known comes from the agencies' own case studies and the press, so read each step as the makers' account of it.

## 1. The brief

Every brief started with something that changed in the business, and the old identity could not carry it. Airbnb had outgrown a logo a founder drew in a few hours. Mailchimp had launched dozens of new products that did not sell on its email reputation. Mastercard was turning from a card company into a digital payments company. The Guardian had to cut its printing costs. The Whitney was moving to a new building.

> Credibility in email marketing wasn't translating elsewhere. — [COLLINS](https://www.wearecollins.com/work/mailchimp), on Mailchimp

Briefs also grow once the work starts. Burger King's was about showing off the quality of its food, and the team was first asked to reuse the existing assets. The audit showed they could not, and it became a full rebrand. The Guardian's print-cost decision became a redesign of the paper, the site, the apps and the masthead.

> It didn't start out as a rebrand brief. — Lisa Smith, [Jones Knowles Ritchie](https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/BN_Podcast_Transcript_Ep_019.pdf), on Burger King

Without a client, the brief is the founder's stance. Liquid Death's founder wrote his own after a client turned down his idea, and since there was no money for ads, the product itself had to be shareable. Radiohead's brief was one sentence from the singer to an art-school friend.

> Our record sleeves are shit, do you want to come and have a go? — Thom Yorke to [Stanley Donwood](https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2025/aug/07/stanley-donwood-interview-radiohead-thom-yorke-art-oxford)

![[bp-whitney-before-after.png|[Whitney](https://whitney.org/): the previous wordmark, and the W that replaced it]]

## 2. Research and insight

Research was immersion and archive work, not surveys. [DesignStudio](https://www.designstudio.com/) sent four people to stay with Airbnb hosts across thirteen cities. [Jones Knowles Ritchie](https://www.jkrglobal.com/) built a Burger King archive from eBay because the company had none. [Michael Bierut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Bierut) audited fifty years of the Mastercard logo. The Whitney handed its designers a cardboard box of old reports and identities.

The insight that came out was always one sentence, and often it was already sitting in the client's own material.

| Brand | The insight |
|---|---|
| Airbnb | Airbnb is about people, not places |
| Mastercard | Under all the technical fixes, two primary colours and the simplest shape, and the company already owns them |
| Whitney | A curator's line: the history of art is not a simple line, which became the zigzag W |
| The Guardian | Readers find stories by topic, not by how the newsroom is organised |
| Liquid Death | The funniest marketing belongs to junk food, while healthy brands lecture |

> It would be much easier to present the history of art as a simplistic line, but that's not the Whitney. — Donna De Salvo, chief curator, in the [studio's essay](http://web.archive.org/web/20170217064852/http://www.jetset.nl/archive/whitney-museum-identity)

Two projects skipped research. Liquid Death tested a fake product instead: a can render and a cheap commercial, posted before the product existed, which built a following of tens of thousands. Radiohead's eras start from an image or an accident.

![[bp-airbnb-belo-sketch.png|[Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/): people, places, love, Airbnb]]
![[bp-whitney-w-sketches.png|[Whitney](https://whitney.org/): early sketches, the W measuring the space around art]]

## 3. Strategy

Purpose is published as one short line, and several brands inherited theirs rather than writing a new one. Airbnb wrote "Belong Anywhere". Mother's is "Make our children proud". Burger King went back to its 1954 line, "Have It Your Way", and Mastercard to Priceless from 1997.

Positioning is defined against the category's habits, not by price. Each brand names what it is not, and almost none names its competitors.

| Brand | Defined against |
|---|---|
| Liquid Death | Water brands that preach; it borrows the look of beer and energy drinks |
| Mailchimp | Software brands gone austere and sterile; it went hand-drawn and surreal |
| The Guardian | The red-top tabloids, in the same tabloid format |
| Mother | Agencies owned by networks; it is not for sale |
| Airbnb | Mass-produced, impersonal travel |
| Mastercard | Card brands; it calls itself a technology company |

Nobody publishes a ranked list of audiences, but most name a second, practical audience next to the customer: Airbnb's hosts and its own design team, Burger King's franchisees, Mastercard's banks and merchants, The Guardian's paying supporters. Liquid Death started with punks and metal fans because they would get the joke, and grew from there.

Personality is stated as a tension between two qualities. Mailchimp is "a precise balance between the sophisticated and the surreal". The Guardian is serious and also witty. Mother is serious about clients' work and never about itself. Burger King wrote four principles and judged every design against them: mouthwatering, big and bold, playfully irreverent, proudly true.

> We don't actually take ourselves seriously. — [Liquid Death](https://liquiddeath.com/pages/faq)

![[bp-mailchimp-outsider-art.png|[Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/): the outsider-art board, sophisticated and surreal]]

## 4. Message and pitch

The core message is a short phrase, and sometimes it is an idea instead of a slogan. Airbnb's is "Belong Anywhere", Liquid Death's "Murder Your Thirst", Mastercard's "There are some things money can't buy". Burger King's creative idea was a single word, craveable. Radiohead has no tagline at all: the artwork is the message.

How the work was sold is public for only four of the nine, and only those four show the routes that lost.

| Brand | How it was sold | Routes that lost |
|---|---|---|
| Whitney | A paid pitch between four studios, then presented to every department and the trustees | A text-only route, a multi-image route and a static monogram |
| Burger King | Five directions from evolution to revolution; the client gravitated to one | A modernised version of the old logo, called synthetic |
| Liquid Death | Investors said it looked too much like beer; the founder launched anyway and sold them on the audience | None, one route only |
| Radiohead | No client: covers taped to the studio kitchen cupboards overnight, and the choice was obvious next morning | Several, from a topiary idea to a paint-squirting Dalek |

For the rest only the argument is public. [Bierut](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/new-mastercard-logo-pentagram/) sold Mastercard as evolution: the new logo looks new but does not make the old one look wrong. COLLINS sold Mailchimp as strategy first, identity second. Airbnb's agency won the pitch by building a fake Airbnb listing in its own studio, and Liquid Death pitched investors with a video and its audience numbers. In both, the pitch was proof that the brand worked.

## 5. Voice

Written voice guides are rare. Two of the nine publish one. Mailchimp's [content style guide](https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone/) is open for anyone to use: plainspoken, genuine, dry humour, and clarity always beats a joke. The Guardian's [style guide](https://www.theguardian.com/guardian-style-guide-a) is a public A to Z. For the rest, the voice is read from the copy.

| Brand | How it sounds |
|---|---|
| Liquid Death | Horror and metal about something harmless; every flavour is a death pun |
| Burger King | Short, food first, cheeky: "So beautiful you'll cry" |
| Mother | Self-mocking family humour |
| Airbnb | First person, story-led, short lines: "Don't go there. Live there." |

Naming rules are the part of voice that does get written down. Mailchimp fixes its name as one word with a small c and forbids banana puns. Mastercard spells its name with a capital in running text even though the logo is lowercase. The Whitney puts its rule in the type: the name in capitals, one weight.

![[bp-mailchimp-billboard.jpg|[Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/): "Bad at future-proof names. Good at more than mail."]]
![[bp-burger-king-billboard.jpg|[Burger King](https://www.bk.com/): short, food first, cheeky]]

## 6. Visual identity

Existing equity was kept more often than it was thrown out. Burger King went back to its bun logos from 1969 and 1994. Mastercard kept its two circles and dropped everything around them, and in 2019 dropped the name too. Mailchimp simplified its chimp, Freddie, rather than replacing him. The Guardian kept its body typeface from 2005. Airbnb is the one clean break, and it could break because there was no designed brand to keep.

> We didn't see these changes going backwards, but going inwards. — Rafael Abreu, [Burger King](https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/BN_Podcast_Transcript_Ep_019.pdf)

![[bp-burger-king-logo-before-after.png|[Burger King](https://www.bk.com/): back to the bun]]
![[bp-mastercard-before-after.png|[Mastercard](https://www.mastercard.com/): the two circles, kept]]
![[bp-mailchimp-logo-before-after.png|[Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/): Freddie, simplified]]
![[bp-airbnb-logo-before-after.png|[Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/): the one clean break]]

The identity is a rule for what stays fixed and what is free. Mailchimp fixed its logo, colour and type and let illustration and photography change freely. The Whitney fixed its name in the upper left corner and let the W change shape to fit every format. Mastercard generates rings and segments from the circles. Mother reweaves its tartan for each office. Radiohead changes medium on purpose with each album. The risk is on record too: within two years, another agency judged Mailchimp's free layer expressive but not scalable.

![[bp-mailchimp-fixed-and-free.png|[Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/): what is fixed, what is free]]
![[bp-whitney-w-chart.png|[Whitney](https://whitney.org/): the W, redrawn for every format]]
![[bp-mastercard-cards.jpg|[Mastercard](https://www.mastercard.com/): rings and segments drawn from the circles]]

Radiohead shows the free end of the scale: no fixed logo beyond a small bear, and a new medium for every album.

![[bp-radiohead-the-bends.png|[Radiohead](https://www.radiohead.com/): The Bends, 1995]]
![[bp-radiohead-ok-computer.png|[Radiohead](https://www.radiohead.com/): OK Computer, 1997]]
![[bp-radiohead-kid-a.png|[Radiohead](https://www.radiohead.com/): Kid A, 2000]]
![[bp-radiohead-in-rainbows.png|[Radiohead](https://www.radiohead.com/): In Rainbows, 2007]]

Colour follows one of three approaches. One hero colour: Mailchimp's yellow, Airbnb's coral, Mastercard's orange. Colour as a code: The Guardian gave each of its five sections a colour, and the Whitney uses colour only to mark things like ticket types. Colour per unit: Liquid Death gives each flavour its own can colour, Mother each office its tartan, Radiohead each album its palette.

![[bp-mailchimp-colours.png|[Mailchimp](https://mailchimp.com/): one hero colour]]
![[bp-the-guardian-pillars.jpg|[The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/): a colour per section]]
![[bp-liquid-death-can-lime.png|[Liquid Death](https://liquiddeath.com/): a colour per flavour]]

Several brands drew their own typeface, and the first one often gets redone. Airbnb's custom face from 2014 was replaced by Airbnb Cereal in 2018, with a taller x-height for small interface text. Mailchimp swapped Cooper Light for a commissioned face two years after launch, because Cooper was weak in the interface. The Guardian had a new headline face drawn because its old one lacked presence at tabloid sizes.

| Brand | Typeface | Own or licensed |
|---|---|---|
| Airbnb | [Airbnb Cereal](https://web.archive.org/web/2018/https://airbnb.design/introducing-airbnb-cereal/), by Dalton Maag | Own, replaced the first custom face |
| Burger King | Flame, by Colophon | Own |
| Mailchimp | Cooper Light, then [Means](https://commercialtype.com/custom/means_for_mailchimp) by Commercial Type | Licensed, then own |
| The Guardian | [Guardian Headline and Titlepiece](https://dandad.org/awards/professional/2018/crafts-for-design/26503/the-guardian-headline-and-titlepiece-fonts), by Commercial Type | Own |
| Mastercard | FF Mark, every letter holding part of a circle | Licensed |
| Whitney | [Neue Haas Grotesk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helvetica), capitals, one weight | Licensed |
| Radiohead | None; a new face each album | Licensed per album |

![[bp-airbnb-cereal-weights.png|[Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/): Cereal, the second custom face]]
![[bp-the-guardian-headline-weights.png|[The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/): Headline, light to black]]
![[bp-burger-king-flame-alphabet.png|[Burger King](https://www.bk.com/): Flame]]
![[bp-mastercard-ff-mark.png|[Mastercard](https://www.mastercard.com/): FF Mark]]

Imagery and motion are the least documented layers. Where there are rules, they are crisp: the Whitney shows one artwork at a time, Burger King runs macro food photography beside flat illustration, Mailchimp works with a roster of named illustrators. Sound appears as events more than systems: Mastercard's sonic logo in 2019, Radiohead's ten-second animations in place of music videos.

![[bp-burger-king-photography.jpg|[Burger King](https://www.bk.com/): macro food photography]]

## 7. Mood

Mood is described in the makers' own adjectives, and almost never in a published moodboard. Mastercard: simple, light, fresh and optimistic. The Guardian: urgent, inclusive and bold. Burger King: juicy, bulging, oozing, mouthwatering. The Whitney is the exception, and its studio lists its references: Op Art, Minimal and Conceptual Art, the supergraphics of the 1960s and 70s.

Mood is often built from a contradiction. Liquid Death's metal menace is undercut by the harmless subject. Mother crosses a warm family home with a raw warehouse. The Guardian lifts serious black and white with colour and wit. Burger King's makers push back on the reading that their look is 1970s nostalgia: it is the colours of the food.

> Don't be scared, it's just water. — [Liquid Death](https://liquiddeath.com/)

## 8. Rollout

The identity starts where it is used most, and old and new live side by side for a while. Airbnb redesigned every page of its site and app. Mastercard started with cards, terminals and checkouts, built to sit beside billions of cards still carrying the old mark. Burger King went digital first, then packaging and uniforms, with restaurant signs last over several years. Liquid Death's can is its media plan. Radiohead's main touchpoint is the sleeve.

Launch day is either one switch or a long, staged reveal.

| Brand | Launch |
|---|---|
| The Guardian | Paper, site and apps all switched on one Monday in January 2018 |
| Airbnb | One day in July 2014: manifesto, film, new site and apps, and a make-your-own-logo tool |
| Whitney | Two years before the new building opened, to carry the museum through the move |
| Mastercard | The new mark in 2016, the name dropped in 2019, a sound added a month later |
| Liquid Death | The brand before the product: a video in 2018, cans on sale in 2019 |

The step every case study leaves out is how staff were told.

## 9. The guide

Seven of the nine have no public brand guide. The two that do, publish for outsiders who must reproduce the brand. Mastercard publishes a short [brand mark guide](https://brand.mastercard.com/content/dam/mccom/brandcenter/assets/mc_brandmark_guidelines_v8,1.pdf) because banks, merchants and partners print its logo. Mailchimp publishes its [logo files](https://mailchimp.com/about/brand-assets/) and its content guide. The rest is internal: the Whitney's manual runs to 220 pages, Airbnb's lives as a private website, Mother mentions an internal bible.

The rules that are quoted are few and short. The Whitney: the name in the upper left, in capitals, one weight, black on white. Mailchimp: always pair the name with Freddie, never alter the files. Radiohead's rules are about process: no undo on OK Computer, a new medium each album.

> Very hard to do bad design out of. — Jones Knowles Ritchie, on the [Burger King system](https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/BN_Podcast_Transcript_Ep_019.pdf)

## 10. Reception

Most projects drew a hostile reading somewhere. Most recovered. Airbnb's Bélo was mocked within a day, and it is unchanged today. Liquid Death was called the stupidest branding of 2019, and then brand of the year. The Whitney won a [D&AD](https://www.dandad.org/) pencil and was called a logo that goes nowhere. Mastercard's was judged not groundbreaking, but done right.

> Not groundbreaking, but it gets the job done, and done right. — [Brand New](https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_identity_for_mastercard_by_pentagram.php), on Mastercard

> The Whitney Museum's new logo goes nowhere. — [Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/the-whitney-museums-new-logo-goes-nowhere/)

![[bp-airbnb-reactions.png|[Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/): the first day's reactions]]
![[bp-whitney-buttons.jpg|[Whitney](https://whitney.org/): the W that "goes nowhere", on buttons]]

Business results are claimed by the agencies and rarely confirmed. Burger King's agency reports billions of impressions and a jump in visit intent. COLLINS credits its Mailchimp work with the company's later sale. The Guardian has the strongest evidence, reaching its break-even target the year after the redesign, though nobody can separate the identity from the cost cutting around it.
