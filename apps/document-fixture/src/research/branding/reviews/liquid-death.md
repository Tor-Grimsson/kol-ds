# Review — Liquid Death branding dossier

Reviewed 2026-10-22 against `src/research/branding/brands/liquid-death.json` (10 found / 9 partial / 1 not-found, 31 sources, validator passes).

## Verdict

**Needs fixes.** The sourcing is strong. Every quote checked against CNBC, TechCrunch, Business Insider, Fortune, The Drum, NBC, High Times, Marketing Dive, Contagious, FoodNavigator, Campaign ME, OMR, Loudwire and the brand's own pages is verbatim and correctly attributed. The pitch story holds: the CNBC figures ($1,500 commercial, 3M views, ~80k followers, $1.6M seed from Science in January 2019) are all confirmed. The problems are small. One wrong fact comes from the dossier's own launch image: "Murder Your Thirst" was not on the foot band in 2019. Two details are mis-attributed: the ad being "banned by Facebook", and a Stereogum citation for a Business Insider quote. One closable gap: Loudwire's article has a second Cessario line saying Carsola did "a lot of our art", which moves imagery and logo forward. Wayback was offline for the whole session (Internet Archive returned "Temporarily Offline"), so the Masters of Scale and Fast Company leads remain untested.

## Wrong facts

1. **core-message summary:** "printed on the foot band of every can since launch" is wrong. The dossier's own `launch-2019-alps-tallboy.jpg` and `launch-2019-12pack.jpg` show the 2019 tallboy with "MURDER YOUR THIRST" as a small line under "MOUNTAIN WATER". On those cans the foot band read "DRINKING WATER FROM THE AUSTRIAN ALPS". The foot-band position belongs to the current 12 oz cans. The first point ("on the can's bottom band (still, sparkling, flavours, tea)") is right for current cans only.
2. **pitch points:** "The waterboarding ad was banned by Facebook after ~3 million views (Wikipedia)". Wikipedia says only "which was later banned", with no platform named. Also, Wikipedia gives "over 100,000 'likes'" where CNBC gives "almost 80,000 followers". Keep CNBC's figure and note the conflict.
3. **insight points:** "a 3D can render and a $1,500 Facebook ad in 2018". The $1,500 was the cost of producing the commercial. The paid media came on top of that ("another 'few thousand dollars in paid media'"). The pitch answer gets this right; insight conflates the two.
4. **brief points:** "bands sponsored by Monster". CNBC says "Monster energy drinks were a sponsor on the tour", not of the bands.
5. **type points / typefaces[1]:** 1000logos compares "the uppercase tagline" to "Demosreg Next or Hawking" (sic). It does not say "the original 'Mountain Water' line", and the face is not "Demos Next". Quote what 1000logos says and keep the mapping to the product line as our reading.
6. **audience example "Niche feel":** the quote "What makes this appealing for such a large group is that it feels like a niche thing" is Cessario to Business Insider. Stereogum only re-quotes it. Cite BI.
7. **purpose point 4:** "5 cents per can in 2019" has no source in that answer. It is supported by The New Yorker ("donate five cents from each one purchased to ocean recovery") and Stereogum ($0.05). Add one of them.
8. **logo example label:** "— Liquid Death statement". Loudwire attributes the words to Cessario in the brand's follow-up post. Relabel it "— Mike Cessario, Liquid Death post, via Loudwire".
