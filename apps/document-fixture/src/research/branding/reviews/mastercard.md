# Review — Mastercard branding dossier

Reviewed 2026-10-22 against `src/research/branding/brands/mastercard.json` (9 found / 11 partial, 22 sources, validator passes).

## Verdict

**Needs fixes.** The dossier is mostly accurate. 53 of the 58 numbered claims check out against their sources. It has two wrong facts: the minimum-size labels and an unsupported Wikipedia claim. Several gap notes are now out of date: the Fast Company 2016 interview is readable on Wayback, and the 2016 launch text survives in Mastercard's Europe press kit. With the Fast Company and AIGA Eye on Design interviews, `insight` can be marked `found`. Six other partials gain material but stay partial.

## Wrong facts

1. **Minimum sizes are mislabelled** (`logo` points). Guide v8.1 p6 gives: Horizontal Brand Mark 45 px / 16.8 mm; **Vertical** Brand Mark 24 px / 8.9 mm; **Acceptance Mark** 34 px / 16.2 mm. The dossier says "24 px (symbol), 34 px (vertical)". In 2016 there was no standalone symbol size.
2. **"Priceless was adopted in 105 countries by 2006 and won a 2006 Effie (Wikipedia)"** (`core-message` points). Neither the 105 countries nor the Effie appears in the cited Wikipedia article. The claim is unsupported.
3. **DNA quote attributed to Dezeen** (`insight` points). Dezeen says Bierut "told FastCoDesign". The original is Fast Company, Diana Budds, 14 Jul 2016, and now readable on Wayback. The same applies to the Fortune-sourced "Nike swoosh" quote.
4. **"Research figure" example is not verbatim** (`insight` examples). Pentagram reads "an astonishing 81 percent of consumers spontaneously recognized the new symbol without the inclusion of the Mastercard brand name". The dossier drops "the inclusion of".
5. **Endline cited to a page with a typo** (`core-message`, `voice` examples). The 2022 Newsroom text reads "There are something things money can't buy." The clean endline is in Wikipedia's infobox and lede.
6. **Gap notes say things that are no longer true.** `insight` and `pitch` say the Fast Company 2016 interview was blocked, but it reads fine on Wayback. `launch` says "the original 14 July 2016 release is no longer online", but Mastercard's Europe digital press kit keeps the 2016 summary and Horizont and Merca 2.0 quote the release.

## Status problems

- **`insight` → found.** The ask wants how they researched and the single insight in their words. Fast Company has the audit: Bierut "took stock of the existing logo and its evolution" and found decades of technical tweaks (drop shadow, zipper "teeth", condensed type). It also has the insight: "underneath this stuff are two of the three primary colors on the wheel and the most simple shape, which is circle. And they own this… That's a gift we were given." AIGA adds the colour method, Chevreul's simultaneous contrast via Albers. With Mastercard's recognition research this is enough. Keep a gap line for the research sample.
- **`brief` (found) is missing the client side of who briefed whom.** Cindy Chastain, SVP customer experience and design: "As we're evolving as a company, this is the right time to evolve our brand identity" (Fast Company). Bierut on the charge: "The charge was to take these basic elements and make them fit the uses they're subjected to today" (AIGA).
- **`elements` (found)** leans partly on the Cannes Lions entry, which couldn't be read. It is still `found` on Pentagram and Creative Review, but the Cannes sentence must be cut or marked unverified.
- **`reception` (partial)** is correctly partial. Awards are still unconfirmed and no business results are attributed. Public reaction is now closable (see below).
- **`pitch`, `positioning`, `audience`, `imagery`, `launch`** stay partial, but each should take the new material below.
- `purpose`, `personality`, `voice`, `motion`: correctly partial. Nothing new found.
- **Palette proportion is the researcher's reading.** Pentagram says orange is "the new primary color" and warm greys are "the base for all compositions". The palette marks red and yellow `dominant` and orange `supporting`, which is true only of the mark. Warm greys appear nowhere in `palette`.
