# Review: Wieden+Kennedy dossier — strategy layer

Reviewed 2026-10-22. Scope: the seven `strategy` answers (brand-idea, audience, brief, design-rationale, campaigns, off-screen, perception), the assets under `public/research/wieden-kennedy/strategy/`, and source types across the whole file (84 unique URLs). The expression layer was reviewed and gated earlier; that review is kept in `_tmp/2026-10-22-reviews-before-strategy/wieden-kennedy.md`.

Quotes were checked against the cited pages. The Brand New Ep. 065 transcript PDF was downloaded and its text extracted, so every quote attributed to it was matched word for word. Campaign US pages return 403; their claims were checked through search snippets of the same articles.

## Verdict

**Needs fixes.** The quoting is unusually clean: 28 of 29 quotes and attributions checked are confirmed at the cited URL, and stated rationale is kept apart from interpretation in `design-rationale`. The faults are elsewhere. `off-screen` says the only exterior branding is the etched Portland doors, but the dossier's own asset shows the W+K London façade with a window wordmark and a giant NOT installation. None of the eight strategy assets are in `assetsIndex`. Several sources are mistyped, most of all the 2011 Global Credentials, which W+K wrote, typed `reference` in eight answers. A small number of secondary claims are mis-dated or over-stated.

## Wrong facts

1. **`off-screen`: "The only exterior branding found is the stacked wordmark etched on the entrance doors."** The dossier's own asset `strategy/not-wk-team-creative-review-2023.jpg` (Creative Review, Jan 2023) shows the W+K London street façade with a "Wieden Kennedy +" lockup on the ground-floor window and a building-height pink inflatable "NOT" installed behind the glazing. That is exterior signage and an environmental launch piece. The claim is true only of Portland, and the answer should say so.
2. **`brief`: "$300M flat revenue in 2023".** Campaign's Agency Performance Review 2023 covers 2022, and the figure is US revenue. Campaign's 2024 review then reports revenue flat at $300M again for 2023. Write "revenue flat at ~$300M (US) in 2022 and 2023 (Campaign APR 2023, 2024)". The same sentence says Bud Light and KFC were "resigned". Campaign's wording is "declining to defend", and its 2022 report card describes KFC as lost after seven years. Use "did not defend".
3. **`campaigns`: "NOT's credentials deck closes with a hostile Brand New comment".** In the transcript Rix says the quote, from a commenter called "RebrandouVer", goes in a section headed "in the words of others" after a client testimonial. He does not say it is the last slide or that the comment was posted on Brand New (Armin mentions comments on Brand New, Instagram and Twitter). Write "the deck's 'in the words of others' section quotes a commenter (RebrandouVer)…".
4. **`audience`: "About copy ('the best work of our lives with inspiring clients')".** This text is not on the visible About page. It is the About page's `seoDescription` in `page-data/about/page-data.json`. Label it "About meta description".

## Thin answers

- **brand-idea**: Strong on quotes. The closing sentence, "In design terms the idea shows up as absence… so the work and the people carry the brand", and "The two culture artefacts are the idea in physical form" are the researcher's interpretation, presented in the same voice as the quotes. Mark them as interpretation, as `design-rationale` does.
- **campaigns**: The ask wants "how they looked". The answer names campaigns but describes the look of none of them, not even "So Win" (black-and-white, Doechii VO). The 2026 items rest on an LBB company directory page, an Owler feed and a Polish tag-index page, not on articles. Cite the actual articles or drop the 2026 line. There are no images of any campaign or of W+K's own award or self-promotion pieces, apart from NOT and the London maker logos.
- **off-screen**: The two artefacts the answer calls the brand's best-known physical signs, the Fail Harder wall and the London mannequin, have no image. The entrance-door photo it cites (Commons, 2012) is not stored under `strategy/`, although it is the only evidence for the Portland signage claim. The London façade and window signage visible in the stored NOT photo is not described at all.
- **perception**: Fine on awards and obituaries. It should state outright that no design-press critique of the master W+K wordmark exists (the gap is implied, not stated), so readers don't take the NOT and maker-logo reviews as verdicts on the core identity.
- **brief** / **design-rationale** (`partial`): Gaps are honestly documented and searched widely. See Closable gaps for a lead the search missed.
- **audience**: No issues beyond Wrong facts #4.

## Closable gaps

- **Previous identity / who drew the logo (`brief`, `design-rationale` gaps).** Search results surface a Graphis entry, "Wieden & Kennedy Logo", credited to C.S. Anderson Design (art director Charles S. Anderson; designers Haley Johnson, Charles S. Anderson) as a silver award: https://graphis.com/entry/0341f35c-4b2d-11e2-a2c9-f23c91dffdec. The URL now redirects to the Graphis homepage, so this is unverified. It is the first named designer credit for any W+K mark and should be chased (Graphis Logo annuals, the CSA Design archive, Wayback copies of the Graphis entry). If it holds, it is the "previous identity" the `brief` gap says is unknown.
- **Allied Works' own project text.** The `belgium-architects.com` page is an architecture-directory mirror. The same text appears on german-architects.com. Look for the original on alliedworks.com (or a Wayback copy) and cite that as `agency`.
- **2025 maker logos.** LBB news (https://lbbonline.com/news/wiedenkennedy-london-celebrates-londons-eclectic-makers-with-logo-design-project) and creativebrief (https://www.creativebrief.com/bite/trend/bite-trend/wieden-kennedy-london-collaborates-with-artists-for-logo-refresh) cover the project. Both are uncited in `brief`, `campaigns` and `off-screen`.
- **KFC loss.** Campaign's Agency Report Cards 2022 (https://campaignlive.com/article/agency-report-cards-2022-wieden+kennedy/1752457) covers the KFC loss after seven years, which the global COO called "brutal". It is a better source for that claim than the 2023 review.
- **Fail Harder, contemporary press.** Mumbrella, "Pin point creativity" (https://mumbrella.com.au/pin-point-creativity-12326), is coverage from the time of the wall. It is a press source independent of the insider Lasser account.

## Strategy problems

- **Quotes**: Every quote checked is said by the named person at the cited URL. The only problems are the context errors in Wrong facts #3 and #4.
- **Rationale vs interpretation**: `design-rationale` keeps the two apart well ("outside interpretation (press): …"). `brand-idea` does not (see Thin answers).
- **Beyond own site**: Yes. Every `found` strategy answer has `press` sources: trade press (Adweek, Campaign, The Drum, Muse), design press (Brand New, Creative Review, It's Nice That, Design Week), local press (Seattle Times, Willamette Week, OPB, KOIN), international press (W&V German, Meio & Mensagem Portuguese, mmponline Polish, Campaign India, Mumbrella, B&T). Missed sources are listed under Closable gaps (Graphis, LBB news, creativebrief, Campaign 2022 report card, Mumbrella 2008).
- **Insider voices typed as press**: Muse by Clios "The Art of Failure" is a first-person piece by Jimm Lasser, a W+K New York creative director. It is fine to cite, but it is an insider account, not outside coverage. The answers already attribute it to Lasser by name, so the only issue is how much weight it carries.

## Source-type problems

All 84 unique source URLs were checked, not a sample of 20.

1. **W+K Global Credentials, Aug 2011 (Wayback)**, typed `reference` in 8 answers (positioning, architecture, origin, brand-guide, message-core, voice-principles, brand-idea, brief). It is a W+K-authored document (Commons lists the author as Wieden + Kennedy, Inc.). It should be `owned`. The Wayback Machine is only where it is hosted.
2. **"Allied Works — Wieden+Kennedy World Headquarters" (belgium-architects.com)** is typed `agency` in design-rationale and off-screen, and `press` in origin and imagery. The same URL has two types. It is the architect's own project text on a directory platform, so use `agency` everywhere, and retitle it "Allied Works project text (via belgium-architects.com)" until the alliedworks.com original is found.
3. **LBB Online — Wieden+Kennedy New York company page** is typed `press` (campaigns). An LBB company page is an agency-maintained profile, not editorial, so it should be `owned`. Better still, replace it with the specific LBB news articles.
4. **Creativepool — "Wieden+Kennedy bring The Kennedys to London"** is typed `press` (audience). It is at `creativepool.com/wk/news/…`, W+K's own Creativepool profile feed, so it should be `owned`.
5. **NYU ITP — "Call: WK12 ad school"** is typed `press` (audience). It is a university opportunities board reposting W+K's recruitment call, not coverage, so it should be `reference` (or `owned` if it reproduces W+K copy verbatim).
6. **mmponline tag page** is typed `press` (campaigns). The type is acceptable, but a tag index is not a citable article. Cite the article.

The other types check out: wk.com, wklondon.com, wktokyo.com, wkams.com, page-data, CSS/JS and Instagram as `owned`; Wikipedia, Commons, Linotype, Fontmeme, B Lab and Owler as `reference`; trade, design and local press as `press`.
