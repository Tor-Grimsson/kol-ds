# Review: foundations / colour-systems

Reviewed file: `src/research/foundations/colour-systems.json` (researchedAt 2026-10-21). Reviewed 2026-10-21 against the cited URLs. Raw sources (Tailwind, Radix, material-web, MCU, Carbon, USWDS, Primer, Open Color, Polaris, Atlassian) were pulled as raw files from GitHub/unpkg; doc pages were fetched as HTML and text-searched. All contrast ratios were recomputed with the WCAG 2 relative-luminance formula.

## Verdict

**Needs fixes.** The data layer is unusually good. Every one of the nine ramps and all four tables match their raw sources hex for hex, and 17 of 17 recomputed contrast ratios come out exactly as stated. The problems are in the interpretation. One token claim is wrong: Carbon's dark-theme link is `$link-primary` = Blue 40, not `$link-inverse`. One arithmetic claim is wrong: Material's "40-tone gap" is actually 60. One quote is fabricated (the Stripe HSL line). The "tone Δ50 guarantees 4.5:1" rule is repeated in three places, but it fails by a hair at the worst case. The Material role-to-tone mapping is presented as current, but it is the 2021 spec, and MCU now ships 2025/2026 specs where light primary is not fixed at tone 40. Polaris is cited as a live system without saying that Polaris React was deprecated in 2025. About a third of the `established` labels are really conventions observed in 2–3 systems. The gaps a working designer hits first are missing or thin: focus-ring colour, a procedure for deriving a UI-safe brand variant, P3 authoring and fallbacks, and data-viz sequential/diverging rules.

### Recomputed contrast ratios (WCAG 2 formula)

All 17 match the JSON to two decimals: Blue 60 #0f62fe/white **5.00**; #0f62fe/#161616 **3.62**; Blue 50 #4589ff/#161616 **5.41**; #303030/white **13.20**; #1868DB/white **5.20**; #0969da/white **5.19**; #1d70b8/white **5.17**; #6750a4/white **6.44**; #d0bcff/#381e72 **7.71**; #0d74ce/#f4faff **4.53**; #113264/#f4faff **12.00**; #0090ff/white **3.26**; #2378c3/white **4.62**; #005ea2/white **6.72**. ### Tone-delta check (L* → Y, worst case over all tone pairs)

Tone Δ40 → min **3.17:1** (OK). Tone Δ50 → min **4.48:1**, at tones 50 vs 100. That is *below* 4.5, so the "guarantee" is a rounding claim. MCU's own doc states it, and the JSON repeats it three times.

## Wrong facts

1. **Carbon dark-theme link token (brand-vs-ui#0, themes#3).** The JSON says "in dark themes … `$link-inverse` is Blue 40" and "`$link-primary` Blue 60 … becomes `$link-inverse` Blue 40". The generated `@carbon/themes` `_themes.scss` shows: in **White**, `link-primary` #0f62fe and `link-inverse` #78a9ff (for links on `$background-inverse`, a dark band *inside* the light theme). In **G100**, `link-primary` is **#78a9ff** and `link-inverse` is #0f62fe. The role keeps its name and the theme re-aliases it to a different step. That is the JSON's own tokens#2 thesis, and these two findings contradict it.
2. **"The 40-tone gap between primary and on-primary in both modes" (themes#0).** Light is 40 vs 100 and dark is 80 vs 20, so the gap is **60** in both modes. 60 is what puts the baseline at 6.44:1 / 7.71:1. A 40-tone gap would only promise 3:1.
3. **Fabricated Stripe quote (colour-spaces#0).** "because its lightness isn't perceptually uniform" does not appear in the article. Stripe's words are "the way HSL calculates lightness is flawed". Either fix the quote or drop the quotation marks.
4. **Material role-to-tone mapping presented as current (themes#0, ramps#2, light-dark-mapping table, brand-ui-split row).** It comes from material-web `v0_192`, which is the 2021 spec. MCU now ships `color_spec_2025.ts` and `color_spec_2026.ts`. In 2025, light-mode `primary` is tone 40 only for the NEUTRAL variant. The default TONAL_SPOT uses `tMaxC(primaryPalette)` (max-chroma tone), and EXPRESSIVE/VIBRANT use tMaxC on both modes. Contrast is enforced by `contrastCurve: getCurve(4.5)` against the surface, not by fixed tones.
5. **"Tone Δ50 guarantees ≥4.5:1" (colour-spaces#2, accessibility#4, magic-numbers table).** At the worst case the minimum is 4.48:1 (tone 50 on 100). Say "≈4.5:1 (4.48 worst case); use Δ≥51 for a hard guarantee" and attribute the rounding to MCU.
6. **Carbon step-count headline (accessibility#5, magic-numbers table).** "6 steps from White for 4.5:1" / "6 steps from White / Black". Carbon's table is per starting colour: Black needs 6 steps, 100 needs 5, 90 needs 4, 80 needs 4. The detail text gets this right, but the headline and the table row assert a single number.
7. **Polaris presented as a current system (brand-vs-ui#1, tokens#1, semantic#0/#2, brand-ui-split table).** npm marks `@shopify/polaris` deprecated ("Polaris React is deprecated and no longer maintained … use Polaris web components"). polaris-tokens 9.4.2 dates from 17 Mar 2025. The values are real but historical.
8. **"HCT chroma tops out at roughly 120" (colour-spaces#2).** This is unsourced and contradicted by MCU ("HCT is infinite … H26 C231 T100"). The practical ceiling depends on gamut and hue.
9. **"Encore (introduced 2019)" (brand-vs-ui#4).** The cited source doesn't say this (see table #29).
10. **Atlassian success ladder (tokens#1 example).** `success{,.subtler,.subtle,.bold}{,.hovered,.pressed}` implies 12 tokens. The file has 10: `subtle` has no hovered/pressed.
11. **Stripe paraphrase (brand-vs-ui#6).** "Of its brand-derived colours only black met small-text contrast" should read "none of the default text colours used for small text except black".

## Mislabelled confidence

1. **accessibility#2 is `contested` but states a verified fact.** README: contested = a popular claim the evidence doesn't support. Either relabel `established` or rewrite the claim as the myth ("APCA is the WCAG 3 contrast method") and keep `contested`.
2. **tokens#0 "three-tier is the norm" → `common-practice`.** The evidence is Primer and Carbon. Radix and Tailwind ship as two tiers (scale → app), and Primer itself says component tokens "are limited and functional tokens are preferred".
3. **tokens#1 naming grammar → `common-practice`.** It describes three systems' conventions, and they differ (the claim's own detail shows three different emphasis ladders).
4. **ramps#5 "production ramps shift hue" → `common-practice`, and scope it to Tailwind.** It rests on one system. Tailwind blue isn't monotonic (300 at 251.8 sits below 50 at 254.6). The "read as rich rather than grey-blue" rationale is unsourced. Carbon/IBM ramps were not checked for drift.
5. **brand-vs-ui#6 "the documented fix" → `common-practice`.** Stripe documents it. GOV.UK and Radix are cited as support but say nothing about moving to a darker step.
6. **themes#1 and themes#3 → `common-practice`.** Lighter-surface elevation and lighter interactive steps in dark mode are conventions from Material/Carbon/Radix, not standards. themes#3 also embeds a "rule of thumb" (opinion) in an `established` finding.
7. **semantic#1 "each status role needs its own mini-ramp" → `common-practice`.** It is a recommendation drawn from two systems.
8. **semantic#3 "data-vis categorical palettes are a … subset of the brand palette" → `common-practice`, and scope it to IBM.** It contradicts semantic#4 (Okabe-Ito is not brand-derived) and the JSON's own Primer data-vis.json5 point.
9. **colour-spaces#4 "P3 is roughly 50% larger" should be marked `contested` or given a range.** Computed from the published primaries, P3's chromaticity area is ~1.36× sRGB in CIE xy and ~1.26× in u′v′. Evil Martians' "30%" sits in that range. WebKit's 50% matches neither, and the JSON states it as fact while also citing 30%.
10. **Unsourced rationales inside `established` findings:** Polaris "deliberate decoupling that keeps merchant content and status colours unambiguous" (brand-vs-ui#1) and "Polaris separates 'warning' and 'caution' for the same reason" (semantic#2). Mark them as inference or drop them.
11. **semantic#4 "a common mistake"** has no source showing it is common. It is fine as `common-practice` only if phrased as a recommendation.

## Missing (with sources)

1. **Focus-ring colour.** This is the biggest gap. The JSON mentions 2.4.13 (AAA) but never says what a designer must ship at AA: 2.4.7 Focus Visible (no contrast number) plus 1.4.11 for the indicator, and 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured. It also misses the two-colour ring technique and how the brand colour relates to the focus colour. Verified evidence: Carbon `$focus` is Blue 60 #0f62fe in White but **#ffffff** in G100. GOV.UK uses a non-brand yellow `focus` #ffdd00 with `focus-text` #0b0c0c. Radix step 7 is "focus rings". Sources: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/css/C40 · https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-visible.html · https://design-system.service.gov.uk/styles/colour/ · https://unpkg.com/@carbon/themes/scss/generated/_themes.scss
2. **Procedure for deriving a UI-safe variant of a brand colour.** The pieces are scattered (Stripe, Leonardo, MCU Contrast) and there is no step list. A step list would be: fix hue, drop OKLCH L (or HCT tone) until the target ratio holds against each surface, re-check chroma in gamut, and keep the exact brand hex for logo/fill only. Material's **SchemeFidelity / SchemeContent** are the documented "keep my brand colour" path ("places the source color in primaryContainer … maintains constant appearance in light mode and dark mode"). That directly undercuts brand-vs-ui#5's claim that brand colours must be pinned or harmonised. Sources: https://github.com/material-foundation/material-color-utilities/blob/main/typescript/scheme/scheme_fidelity.ts · https://github.com/adobe/leonardo/tree/main/packages/contrast-colors
3. **P3 / wide-gamut handling in practice.** It covers what P3 is but not how to ship it: `@media (color-gamut: p3)` and `@supports` fallbacks, hex fallback ordering, the DTCG `hex` fallback field, CSS Color 4 gamut mapping, and relative colour syntax / `color-mix()` for deriving states from a brand token. It also skips the Display-P3 brand-colour problem: a P3-only brand green has no sRGB twin, so print/sRGB specs diverge. Sources: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/color-gamut · https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/CSS_colors/Relative_colors · https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/color_value/color-mix · https://www.w3.org/TR/css-color-4/
4. **Dark-mode brand colour adjustment.** The section covers links and surfaces, not the brand fill itself. Verified cases: Polaris flips its brand fill from #303030 to #ffffff in dark (same 9.4.2 file). Carbon keeps button Blue 60 but moves links to Blue 40 and focus to white. MCU's 2025 spec shifts primary tone per variant. Apple HIG dark mode: 4.5:1 minimum, "strive for … 7:1" for custom colours. Sources: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode · https://unpkg.com/@shopify/polaris-tokens@9.4.2/dist/css/styles.css
5. **Data-viz palette rules beyond categorical.** Sequential, diverging (neutral midpoint) and highlight scales, light-to-dark ordering, and the 1.4.11 nuance that adjacent-segment contrast can be relaxed when values are labelled directly. Sources: https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/which-color-scale-to-use-in-data-vis · https://colorbrewer2.org/ · https://carbondesignsystem.com/data-visualization/color-palettes/ (sequential + alert sections exist but are not used)
6. **State layers as an alternative to per-state ramp steps.** M3 overlays the role colour at fixed opacities: hover 0.08, focus 0.12, pressed 0.12, dragged 0.16 (raw `_md-sys-state.scss`). Carbon instead uses discrete half-step hover tokens. This is a real fork for how a brand colour gets its hover/pressed states. Source: https://github.com/material-components/material-web/blob/main/tokens/versions/v0_192/_md-sys-state.scss
7. **Alpha scales.** Radix ships `blueA` etc. for colour over unknown backgrounds. The ramp-systems table mentions "+ alpha" but nothing explains when to use them. Source: https://github.com/radix-ui/colors/blob/main/src/light.ts
8. **Brand colour across media.** IBM publishes HEX/RGB/PMS/CMYK per swatch. The topic is "brand vs UI overlap", yet there is nothing on reconciling a Pantone/CMYK brand spec with the screen ramp step. Source: https://www.ibm.com/design/language/color/
9. **Better CVD prevalence source.** Okabe-Ito's percentages are a 2002-era summary. Birch (2012), "Worldwide prevalence of red-green color deficiency", JOSA A 29(3):313, doi:10.1364/JOSAA.29.000313, is the primary literature.
