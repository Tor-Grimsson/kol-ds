Takeaways for a branding project

  1. Write the brand idea down in one sentence before the identity brief goes out. Only Rijksmuseum and Sanity could be read back to a stated idea; Vercel's and W+K's had to be assembled from other statements, and Cloudflare's lives partly with its agency.
  2. Tie the identity to a business event and put a date on it. Vercel, Sanity, the Rijksmuseum and Cloudflare all changed identity with a rename, funding round, reopening or production problem; it gives the change a story the press and staff can repeat.
  3. Pick one colour decision and own it by area, not by a long palette. Four of five core palettes fit in a sentence; Cloudflare's 20.5% orange and Vercel's pure monochrome show opposite ways to be unmistakable.
  4. Hold hierarchy with size, tracking and grey value before weight or colour. Vercel, Sanity and W+K all keep display type at 300-450 and tighten it with size; the only brand using bold uppercase headings (Rijksmuseum) pairs it with a custom face.
  5. If you can afford one custom asset, make it the typeface. Geist and the Rijksmuseum family are the two that carry through every surface from signage to web and spread beyond the brand (Geist via OFL and Google Fonts).
  6. Design one signature device that can be rebuilt from rules. Cloudflare's flare has "never complete, max two arcs, crop 20%"; Vercel's guide has "+" intersections; Sanity's textures are tokenised. Devices with written rules survive other people's hands.
  7. Publish the logo rules and the voice rules, even short ones. Only one brand has full logo rules and two have voice principles; the gap is cheap to close (clearspace, minimum size, three traits) and is where the guide-versus-site drift starts.
  8. Keep the guide and the live site in the same file. Cloudflare, Sanity and Rijksmuseum each ship tokens or a guide that no longer matches production; publishing the guide beside the tokens, as Vercel does with its Geist pages and design.md, gives drift fewer places to start.
  9. State an accessibility target and measure against it, including where you fail. The Rijksmuseum's "24 of 55" is the most useful accessibility document in the set; brands that say nothing (W+K) leave computed failures like 3.95:1 grey-on-grey unflagged.
  10. Plan for the logo to outlast the website, and for the positioning line to move. Marks lasted 14 to 16 years across the set while homepage copy at Cloudflare, Sanity and Vercel already shows two positioning lines at once; do not let the logo carry words that will go stale.
