Brand idea, audience and brief

The first strategy questions (brand idea, audience, brief) were hardest to answer from public sources. The idea is rarely written down as an idea; it has to be assembled.

Few brands publish a "brand idea"; most have a mission plus an agency's concept, and the two rarely say the same thing.

Cloudflare's constant is a mission, "help build a better Internet" (partial), while the 2020 creative concept, "boldly go", is the agency-side creative director's account, not Cloudflare's published position. Vercel (found) has no single written idea: the researcher assembled it from the 2020 rename post ("develop, preview, and ship their apps faster, and with confidence"), Basement's account ("Vercel is all about performance. Their brand had to match.") and the August 2026 design.md ("The target is Vercel judgment, not Vercel decoration."). W+K (found) has none for the company; its organising idea is Dan Wieden's maxims ("The work comes first", "Don't act big"). Sanity and the Rijksmuseum are the clearest because their designers stated it: Bleed's "where there is chaos, there must be structure", and Irma Boom's "a national museum with international appeal."

Sources: Cloudflare: Allati, 2020 · Rijksmuseum: Brand New · Sanity: Bleed · Vercel: ZEIT is now Vercel · W+K: Seattle Times, 1992

Two brands state the idea in words and then let the design carry it by absence or by contrast.

Vercel's researcher reading (labelled as such in the answer, not a stated position) is that the idea is carried by absence: monochrome, one solid triangle, an owned typeface, no mascot. Sanity does the opposite: a hand-drawn "expressive and unpolished" symbol placed against "sharp edges and a rigorous typographic framework." Both are single-sentence design strategies, and both are recoverable from the visual output.

Audiences are almost always more than one, and the brands that are honest about it rank them.

Cloudflare's 2025 Content Style Guide opens with "Cloudflare is for everyone" and then defines seven groups (free and pay-as-you-go, contracted, consumers, developers, press, shareholders, policymakers). Sanity's brief named two: "the executive-level marketer and the uncompromising developer." The Rijksmuseum's own annual reports give the actual split: 62% of 2,406,210 visitors in 2024 came from abroad, 64% in 2025, while the stated priorities are young adults and families. Vercel's audience widened in three published steps, from "11M JavaScript developers" (2020) to front-end teams to, by 2026, "people and agents" together. W+K names two and ranks neither: clients and talent.

Sources: Cloudflare: Content Style Guide 2025 · Rijksmuseum: Annual report 2024 · Sanity: Bleed · Vercel: Contrary Research · W+K: wk.com/contact
