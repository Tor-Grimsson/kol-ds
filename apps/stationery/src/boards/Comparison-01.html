Branding Research — Brand comparison

Five brands compared

How to read this: five brands answered the same 39 questions. This page reads them across, by theme, instead of brand by brand. Every claim is from the answer data; each finding links the answer's own sources. Status is the answer's own: found, partial or not-found. No answer in the set is marked contested; disagreements between sources are written inside the answers and are noted where they matter. Measured values come from live CSS and HTML read in October 2026, so they describe the sites on that date.

The brands: Cloudflare (connectivity cloud, 2009), Rijksmuseum (national museum, identity by Irma Boom 2012), Sanity (content platform, identity by Bleed 2025), Vercel (developer cloud, renamed 2020) and Wieden+Kennedy (independent ad agency, 1982). Two are developer platforms in the same neighbourhood, one is a cultural institution, one is an agency. That spread is the point: where all five agree, it is probably general.

Summary

  • Identities change when the business changes, not when the taste does. Vercel renamed with a $21M Series A (2020); Sanity launched its new identity the same day as the Content Operating System and an $85M Series C (8 May 2025); the Rijksmuseum identity went up on the building for the 2013 reopening; Cloudflare's 2016 flattening was about t-shirts and stickers. W+K's wordmark has not been redrawn since at least 2011, and no brief for it is public.
  • Four of five brands have a one-colour or no-colour core, and the fifth uses its colour as an area, not an accent. Vercel is black, white and ten grays; W+K is black and white; Rijksmuseum is slate with under 2% red; Sanity is near-black with one orange (3.2% of pixels on the homepage); Cloudflare commits an orange card that is 20.5% of the page.
  • Type does the hierarchy, and it does it by size and tracking, not weight. Vercel (400-450, -6% tracking), Sanity (400, -0.04em) and W+K (300 everywhere) all keep headings light; Rijksmuseum is the one that goes bold and uppercase. Two brands own a custom typeface (Vercel's Geist, Rijksmuseum's nine-font family); the others license.
  • The published rulebook is thin and uneven. Only Cloudflare publishes a full brand guide; Vercel publishes a shorter one. Voice principles are published by two of five, logo rules fully by one. Four of five publish a UI design system; the agency publishes none.
  • Brand guides and live sites drift apart. Cloudflare's guide says Inter and Tangerine #F6821F; the site runs STK Bureau Sans and #ff5e1f. Sanity's own logo package calls its colours "placeholder" 17 months after launch. Rijksmuseum's styleguide colour names appear nowhere in its CSS.
  • Each brand has one signature device beyond logo, colour and type, and three of the five are visible construction. Cloudflare's flare arcs, Vercel's hairline grid with "+" marks, Sanity's 36 dot and line textures; against Rijksmuseum's full-width wordmark and W+K's plus sign.
  • Marketing happens at events and in client work, not in classic ads. Vercel and Sanity run conferences and launch weeks; the Rijksmuseum's biggest moments were sponsor-driven (ING, Albert Heijn); W+K's marketing is its clients' ads. Only Cloudflare ran named brand ad campaigns (2018, 2020).
  • Outside critique of identities is scarce. Only the Rijksmuseum got real design-press criticism ("fine design. But nothing more."). Sanity has award juries, Vercel has forum reaction, Cloudflare has coverage of its lava lamps, W+K is judged as an agency.
