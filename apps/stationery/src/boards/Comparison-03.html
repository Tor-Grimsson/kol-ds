Identity briefs are published only for the edges of a brand, and they are almost always about a business shift or a practical failure.

Cloudflare (partial) published three briefs, one per identity era; none covers the current site look. The 2016 one was practical: the gradient logo was "really hard to reproduce on t-shirts, signs, stickers" (Cloudflare blog). Sanity (found) repositioned from "open-source headless CMS" to "Content Operating System"; CEO Magnus Hillestad: "I think we're not a headless CMS" (CMS Critic). The Rijksmuseum (partial) needed an identity for a 13 April 2013 reopening after a ten-year closure, replacing a Studio Dumbar logo that had run 32 years; no museum brief is public. Vercel (found) had no single brief: three published steps from the 2020 rename onward. W+K (partial) has no brief for its master wordmark at all; the briefs on record are for its edges, such as the 2023 NOT Wieden+Kennedy design studio, whose problem was reputation as only "an advertising specialist."

The positioning line is moving for the two platform brands, and the moves are visible on the site.

Cloudflare's homepage title is "Build for the agent era" while its press boilerplate still says "connectivity cloud". Sanity's title says "Content Operating System for the AI era" while the H1 on the same page says "The Content Operations Platform". Vercel says "Agentic Infrastructure", with leftover "Frontend Cloud" copy on its Next.js page. Rijksmuseum and W+K, with stable categories, state theirs plainly ("the national museum of the Netherlands"; "an independent creative network").

Sources: Cloudflare: cloudflare.com · Sanity: sanity.io · Vercel: vercel.com/about

Who made it, and how long it lasts

Identity authorship splits three ways: agency, in-house with an agency partner, and unpublished.

Rijksmuseum (found) and Sanity (partial) are clean agency jobs: Irma Boom with Bold Monday's Paul van der Laan, and Bleed (Oslo). Vercel (partial) is in-house (a design team listed at roughly 28 designers and design engineers) with Basement Studio as a long-running partner; the name came from Lexicon Branding. Cloudflare (partial) is the hybrid: Lindon Leader drew the 2010 logo, the 2016 redraw is credited to "our design team", and the 2020 refresh was led by Allati El Henson as creative director. W+K (partial) is the outlier: no designer or studio is credited for its wordmark, and no launch date exists.

Sources: Cloudflare: Rejected logos · Rijksmuseum: Bold Monday · Sanity: Bleed · Vercel: Basement · W+K: Wikimedia Commons

Marks outlive systems: the logo structure rarely changes while the website, palette and type do.

Cloudflare's cloud-and-flare structure is unchanged since 2010, though redrawn brighter in 2026 (#FF5F08 / #FF9910). Vercel kept the ZEIT triangle through the 2020 rename. W+K's stacked Univers wordmark was already in an August 2011 credentials PDF. The Rijksmuseum wordmark dates from 2012. Websites move faster: Cloudflare's live site has already swapped typeface and orange without its guide following (see The read).
