Off screen, the memorable artefacts are functional or architectural, not decorative.

Cloudflare's best-known physical artefact is a working one: the lobby wall of 100 lava lamps feeds the random-number generator behind its encryption (Fast Company, 2017). The Rijksmuseum's identity sits on the building (unveiled on the façade in August 2012), four of nine custom fonts were drawn for signage, and Irma Boom Office still designs "all the Rijksmuseum's graphic output". W+K's brand lives in its Portland HQ (a 1908 warehouse converted by Allied Works, opened 2000) and in artefacts like the "Fail Harder" pushpin wall; there is no branded merchandise. Vercel's off-screen life is event environments and a store ("Objects of Craft": jackets $325, tees $40). Sanity's brief required it to work "like godt i digitale grensesnitt som på fysiske flater" (as well on digital interfaces as on physical surfaces).

Architecture: one master brand wins

Every brand is a master brand, and the sub-brand question is answered by how much logo each product gets.

Sanity: products are plain names with no logo, colour or lockup; only the query language GROQ has its own mark. Rijksmuseum (partial): "Rijksmuseum" plus a noun, no sub-brand logos; its digital product Rijksstudio (2012) was retired, replaced by Collection Online on 26 Nov 2024. W+K: ten offices are tagged with a plus and a code (+PDX, +AMS), though several run their own sites with their own design. Cloudflare: the name prefixes most products (Cloudflare Workers) while coined names stand alone (R2, D1, Pingora); live CSS gives product categories their own colour tokens (compute #0a95ff, security #b52831, ai #00bd7d). Vercel is the sub-brand maximalist: six marked brands on one guidelines page (Vercel, Next.js, Turbo, v0, eve, AI SDK), each with its own logo pack.
